## What changed and why

An earlier review missed the boundary between private evidence and public writing. The published copies included information that readers did not need. That was an editorial error. The full incident details stay in the private record; repeating them here would repeat the error.

The standard now explicitly forbids labeling a person or company as a former, inactive or no-longer client in public content. Historical examples can describe verified work and its date. Approved link choices do not require publishing private reasons. Review all delivery surfaces: article body, embedded instructions, downloads, public review notes, meta articles and generated run data.

The link recipe retains its eight ordered steps, scoped inputs, unknown-access treatment, concept coverage, factual-source rules and full-audit handoff. All five written instruction requirements remain satisfied for the changed revision. This does not certify actual setup, every article requirement or a completed client task. Contributor status remains needs-work.
