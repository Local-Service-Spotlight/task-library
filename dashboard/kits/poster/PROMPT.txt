Make an 18 x 24 inch personal brand poster for [SUBJECT NAME] ([THEIR WEBSITE]). It should tell their real story, and every fact on it must be one a stranger can check.

Use the free kit at https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/kits/poster/ : poster-template-18x24.html, render_poster.py, facts-template.csv, photos-template.csv and QA-CHECKLIST.md. The full recipe is the Task Library task "make-a-personal-brand-poster".

Work in this order and stop to show me your work after steps 3 and 6.

1. Collect the public record. Use only sources you can open: their own site and About page, their company's site and team page, their media and speaking lists, their published posts and books. Write down every URL.

2. Pick a theme from their real story, not a generic one. For example, a former pilot who now coaches sales teams could get a flight log, and a chef who now owns restaurants could get a menu. If no true theme exists, use a plain one. Never invent.

3. Fill facts.csv, one row per printed fact: the exact text, who is making the claim (subject, counted, reported, third-party or design), the source URL, what the source says, the date you checked, and status verified.
   - Keep the company's published claims apart from numbers you counted yourself.
   - Remove duplicates before counting. An episode posted as audio and as video counts once, and a short clip is not an interview.
   - Match titles to their own About page. When sources disagree, note it.
   - Label results that clients reported to them as their report, not audited case studies.
   - Use the narrowest verb the source supports for each person ("Had him on [show]"). Never "friend" or "mentor" unless the source says so.
   - Drop anything you cannot source, even if it sounds good.

4. Fill photos.csv. Before a photo goes on the poster, record who owns it, where the permission is kept, the exact file and its original pixel width. A photo found on a public post is not permission. Use the client's own photos or get the owner's written OK. Never pull photos of other public figures off the web. Ask the client's team for photos they own.

5. Render a proof: python3 render_poster.py --template poster-template-18x24.html --facts facts.csv --photos photos.csv --subject "[NAME]" --deliverable "[POSTER NAME]" --version 1 --date [YYYY-MM-DD] --draft
   Fix every FAIL. Read each WARN at 100 percent zoom.

6. Render the final without --draft. It must pass: exactly 18 x 24 in (1296 x 1728 pt), fonts embedded, no text under 9 pt, text at least 0.5 in from every edge, photos at 150 ppi or more counted on the original pixels, and a PDF title with the version and date. Every new version gets a new number. Never overwrite an earlier file.

7. Give me the PDF, the preview PNG, the QA JSON and the fact sheet, plus a list of anything the client's team must confirm, such as name spellings and photo permissions. Do not send anything to print until the client approves the proof.

8. Write a short run record: what you made, every version and what changed in it, the checks and their results, what is still open, and who acts next.
