# Set up remarketing ads for landing page abandoners

Some people leave your site before they ask for help. A useful follow-up may answer what they still need to know. This guide helps you check who can be reached and whether a small test makes sense.

**The path:** Checked visit signal → eligible audience → useful follow-up → measured result.

**Use this when:** A defined landing page has eligible visitor data and the campaign proposes a bounded follow-up test.

## Inputs
- Exact landing page and actual conversion definition, including confirmed event or destination rather than assuming a thank-you URL.
- The selected platform’s tracking and audience source, permissions, consent/policy requirements and current eligibility.
- Approved follow-up creative, goal/target, supported budget controls, total amount and dates; actual campaign setup authority.

## First-run prompt

> Check whether this landing-page visitor follow-up is feasible. Define visit and conversion-exclusion rules, read actual audience eligibility and select a relevant time window. Use only the platforms and budget already in scope. Do not broaden a too-small audience or call every visitor an identified lost customer.

## Steps
1. Define the audience honestly: people with the recorded visit signal and no recorded conversion under the chosen rule. Some may have converted offline or through an unmeasured path; “abandoner” is a measurement label, not knowledge of each person’s intent.
2. Check the actual visit and conversion event path under [Digital Plumbing](https://blitzmetrics.com/digital-plumbing/). A confirmed conversion event can support exclusion without a separate thank-you page. Installing a Meta pixel is not a universal prerequisite for a Google-only plan.
3. Use only the selected platform and permitted data source. Follow its actual consent, eligibility and restricted-offer rules. Google’s website-audience rules support included and excluded actions; do not upload or expose private customer records to fill a gap.
4. Choose a membership window that matches the offer’s buying cycle and the campaign plan. One day is a short recency-test option from the source, not a universal cutoff after which all intent is gone. Record the exclusion duration and any expected reporting lag.
5. Read the audience’s real serving eligibility. Google’s current guide uses active-user requirements that differ from raw analytics visitor counts; a small one-day group may not be able to serve. An insufficient group stays pending unless the existing scope supports a different relevant window or plan.
6. Choose proof or an answer that addresses a real unresolved question, with rights and claim checks. Avoid personal wording that implies you know an individual visited the page. Do not assume a testimonial is always the right follow-up.
7. For a feasible authorized test, set the actual platform budget level and dates, including all commitments. Google budgets are campaign-level. Read back inclusion/exclusion, any audience expansion and the budget before calling it a remarketing-only test.
8. Check early delivery and frequency, then review matched cost and useful outcomes against the goal. Remarketing is not guaranteed to be the cheapest channel. If it performs poorly, inspect tracking, audience, offer and delivery evidence before blaming the page or automatically rotating creative.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] Visit/no-recorded-conversion rules and their limits are documented.
- [ ] Platform-specific tracking, data rights and actual eligibility pass or remain pending.
- [ ] Window and converter exclusion fit the buying cycle and supported controls.
- [ ] Budget, dates and audience-only claims match saved settings.
- [ ] The result is measured against the goal without guaranteed cheapest-conversion or causal claims.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example:** A shop’s quote page has 12 eligible recorded visitors in a day, but the selected Google audience does not meet the current serving requirement. The operator records the one-day plan as pending rather than reporting 12 reachable prospects. A longer relevant window may be proposed within the actual campaign’s scope; it is not silently substituted. Separately, the conversion rule uses the confirmed form-success event, so no extra thank-you page is invented. No ad is launched merely to test whether the audience might work.
