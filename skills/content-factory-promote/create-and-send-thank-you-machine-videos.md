# Create and send Thank You Machine videos

Someone helped you, and you want to say thanks in a personal way. This guide helps you make a short video that names what they did. Start with the real moment and who should receive the message.

**The path:** Real moment → personal recording → scoped delivery → observed response.

**Use this when:** A verified referral, review, useful contribution or milestone deserves a personal acknowledgment.

## Inputs
- The exact person, factual trigger and appropriate destination, plus the scope for a private message, public post, tag or later reuse.
- The real speaker and recording device, approved facts/voice, and permission for any client details or third-party media.
- Access to the specific authorized sending/posting channel and a tracker for the recording, sent copy and any response. Paid promotion is separate scope.

## First-run prompt

> Prepare a brief thank-you from these facts: who helped, what they did and why it mattered. Have the actual speaker record it; do not invent their experience. Use only the authorized destination and send/share scope, verify any sent copy, and record the response as unknown until observed.

## Steps
1. Verify the trigger in its source record and choose the right recipient. Name a concrete contribution rather than generic praise. Do not call a person a client, mentor or close friend unless that relationship is supported.
2. Write a short recording brief with the person’s name, what happened, why it mattered and a sincere close. Aim for about a minute; do not force filler or invent business results to hit exactly 60 seconds.
3. Have the actual person record in their own voice. Keep it natural, with readable framing and a quiet setting. An agent can prepare the brief and process the file; it must not fabricate a human recording or first-person experience.
4. Check the file, captions and public/private details before delivery. Keep agent playback muted at volume zero. If spoken accuracy cannot be checked silently from a reliable transcript or by the speaker, record that review gap.
5. For an already authorized private send, verify the exact thread/recipient and send the agreed file or accessible link. Read back the sent copy. A prepared message, queued upload and delivered message are different states; do not resend from uncertainty alone.
6. Publish or tag the recipient only when that specific public use is in scope and the content is appropriate to share. A private thank-you does not automatically become public content. A recipient is free not to reply or reshare.
7. Consider derivatives only when useful, permissioned and authorized. Your thanks to someone is not their testimonial about your service. Any paid boost requires its own proven-content, audience and budget checks; it is not a mandatory part of saying thanks.
8. Save the trigger, actual recording, delivery state and any observed response. Route a future content idea or follow-up to its owner without manufacturing a testimonial, referral or new relationship claim.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] Trigger, recipient and relationship wording are supported by the source.
- [ ] The actual speaker’s recording and factual message are used.
- [ ] Private send, public post/tag, reuse and paid promotion each match existing scope.
- [ ] Any send/post has a read-back receipt; unknown response stays unknown.
- [ ] The result is not represented as a recipient endorsement or guaranteed referral.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example:** A shop owner thanks a volunteer who helped arrange a community repair day. The brief says exactly what the volunteer did and why it helped. The owner records 48 seconds. The authorized scope is one private email, so the operator verifies the recipient and sent copy; there is no public tag or boost. A reply two days later says the video was appreciated. The log records that reply, not a testimonial about the shop’s repair quality or a new sale.
