# Sequence content: cold → warm → conversion

A new reader may need an answer before they are ready to buy. A returning reader may need proof or a clear next step. This guide helps you match your posts to those needs.

**The path:** Introduction → useful depth → fitting offer → checked stage results.

**Use this when:** Several source posts have proven useful and the campaign needs a clear path across awareness, engagement and conversion.

## Inputs
- Actual posts with source links, rights and evidence; the real offer and destination.
- Eligible current audience sources/rules with time windows, consent and size/availability limits.
- Goal and metric for each used stage, total budget, action scope, dates and existing campaign records.

## First-run prompt

> Map the approved posts to what each audience needs next. Show actual stage metrics and supported audience rules, with missing eligibility visible. Keep the default nurture path without claiming all strangers must be excluded from every offer. Apply only the authorized setup and record actual delivery separately.

## Steps
1. Read each post and give it a primary job: introduce why it matters, explain how the work helps, or offer a relevant next action. The owned WHY/HOW/WHAT method is useful context; do not force a piece into a stage solely from its title.
2. Build the content map with the source URL, stage, intended audience, result and actual next link. Missing proof or offer material remains a production gap; do not invent case studies to fill a sequence.
3. List eligible audience sources for the intended path. Page engagement, supported video views and site events can have different rules. A pixel is not a universal prerequisite for an entirely on-platform engagement path.
4. Read actual inclusion, exclusion and expansion behavior. Keep a suggested audience distinct from a strict boundary. Do not promise an automatic escalator that captures and retargets every person who saw a post.
5. Use the source’s nurture path as the default: useful introduction, relevant depth, fitting offer. A specific high-intent goal may justify a direct offer to new people; document the reason instead of declaring all such ads invalid.
6. Allocate only stages that the approved plan and eligible audiences can support. Use small tests where feasible and count all commitments. No rule requires a tiny account to fund three active stages before any one can be useful.
7. Verify the configured stage map and actual saved settings. Where actions are authorized, check delivery and source/offer links without playing media aloud. Report created audiences, eligible audiences and actual stage delivery separately.
8. Review the matching stage metrics on the agreed cycle. Low warm-audience growth may reflect eligibility or consent as well as content. A weak conversion stage may involve measurement, audience or offer. Diagnose before asserting a cause, then name one supported next step.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] Each source piece has a real primary job and suitable next step.
- [ ] Audience eligibility and expansion limits are explicit.
- [ ] The path does not promise universal viewer capture or automatic movement.
- [ ] Funded stages fit actual budget/scope and have matched metrics.
- [ ] The review distinguishes possible causes and actual delivery evidence.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example:** A shop maps its founder story to introduction, a repair demonstration to depth and a tune-up page to the offer. The platform reports too few eligible site visitors for the intended offer audience, though it has enough on-platform engagement for a depth test. The map records that exact limit. It does not call the founder clip a failure because the site audience is small. A separate high-intent search visitor may still see the tune-up offer under the existing search plan.
