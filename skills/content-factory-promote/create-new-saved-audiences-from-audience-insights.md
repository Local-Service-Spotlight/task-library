# Create new saved audiences from Audience Insights

Your reports may show a group you have not served well yet. This guide helps you turn that clue into a clear test plan. Start with what the report can really show, not a guess about each person.

**The path:** Dated insight → relevant hypothesis → saved settings → bounded next test.

**Use this when:** A campaign needs a new relevant audience hypothesis and current aggregate reporting provides a useful clue.

## Inputs
- Actual Page/ad reporting with dates, metric definitions and available aggregate breakdowns.
- The tested audience/creative map, real service area and goal.
- Access to inspect or save supported audience settings; relevant permissions and eligibility for any first-party source. A saved audience does not authorize spend.

## First-run prompt

> Review these aggregate audience reports and propose a relevant new audience test. Label missing or suppressed data, read current supported settings and distinguish suggestions from controls. Save only within the supplied scope and return the exact definition, version and next decision; do not launch it automatically.

## Steps
1. Open the exact current reports and note their source, date range and metric. Some demographics, interests or page affinities may not be available. Do not recreate a retired Audience Insights screen or infer individual identities from aggregate results.
2. Compare observed useful engagement or customer outcomes with the current targeting plan. A demographic skew among viewers is not proof that other people are poor customers, and a small sample may not support a change.
3. Choose a specific business hypothesis, such as an adjacent area the business actually serves or a relevant available topic. Do not turn every age or gender pattern into exclusions, especially where the offer’s ad category restricts targeting.
4. Read the current controls and any automated expansion labels. A saved collection of suggestions is not an exact membership list. Keep warm/custom audience sources separate from a saved targeting preset.
5. Record a clear name and definition, such as service-area-topic-date, without embedding private customer names. Save the source report, settings, source rights and size/eligibility status. An estimate is not guaranteed delivery at $1/day.
6. If saving is authorized, create or update only the intended preset and read it back. Check for an existing equivalent before creating a duplicate. Keep active campaign targeting unchanged unless that distinct action is in scope.
7. Queue a proposal for a bounded switch test against an appropriate baseline. A preset may legitimately stay unused while budget, evidence or scope is pending; it is not a failed guide run because no ad launched.
8. Review prior test history before retiring a preset. Mark an outdated hypothesis or unavailable setting in the log; do not delete a shared audience used by active campaigns or declare a segment permanently bad from one weak test.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] The insight comes from a dated available report with sample limits.
- [ ] The hypothesis fits the offer and service area without unsupported exclusions.
- [ ] Preset, custom audience, suggestion and strict control are distinct.
- [ ] Saved settings are named, deduplicated and read back within scope.
- [ ] The next test proposal has a goal and budget decision; it is not an automatic launch.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example:** A shop’s report shows useful visits from Maple Town, where it also provides service. The operator checks the current settings and saves a Maple Town preset under the authorized setup scope. An interest is marked as a suggestion because the interface can expand it. The preset’s estimate is not called a known list of customers. Its test remains queued until the existing campaign owner assigns a budget and date. No active Cedar Town campaign is overwritten.
