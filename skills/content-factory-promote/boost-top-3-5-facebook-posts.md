# Boost top 3–5 Facebook posts

Some of your posts helped people more than others. This guide helps you choose a few to test with a small ad budget. Start with the real posts and their recent results.

**The path:** Recent results → qualified short list → small tests → checked verdicts.

**Use this when:** The Post stage has produced usable organic work and the owner wants a bounded Facebook amplification batch.

## Inputs
- The actual Page’s recent organic post log, ideally the last 30 days, with URLs, format and metrics.
- Ad account roles, original-post eligibility, media/claim rights, and a goal with its target.
- Eligible relevant audiences, currency, minimums, total budget, dates and scope for any launches or stops.

## First-run prompt

> Rank these Facebook posts against the Page’s comparable baseline. Pick up to three to five that fit the goal and have usable rights. Price each actual supported test and carry out only authorized actions. Return post/ad IDs, dates, cost and due review; do not fill the list with weak posts.

## Steps
1. Read the available 30-day organic log and disclose any shorter or missing history. Separate paid amplification from organic response. Compare like formats instead of ranking every post by raw reach.
2. Read shares, real questions, substantive comments and meaningful video metrics. Keep the goal in view: a funny post can be popular without helping the intended buyer.
3. Shortlist up to three to five posts with evidence and rights. Use fewer if fewer qualify. Preserve proof and relationship limits; a famous face or co-appearance alone does not support a client endorsement.
4. Choose relevant warm audiences first when they are eligible and fit the goal, as the source method recommends. A warm label does not prove enough people exist or make a mismatched audience right for the post.
5. Verify the original post in the supported preview, including captions, crops and destination. Use current platform options and record any limitation rather than guaranteeing every post can be boosted.
6. Plan the nominal $1/day for seven days where supported. Price all rows and check true budget limits. A staged one-per-day sequence and a parallel batch have different overlapping commitments and due dates.
7. Launch only within the supplied scope, read back settings and monitor early delivery/ceiling errors. Preserve a stable full-window comparison while allowing protective actions already authorized.
8. At each due date, stop rows that fail the agreed rule and identify qualified winners. About 90% retirement is a portfolio expectation, not a forced quota. A winner can be considered for $30 total over 30 days or a separately bounded increase; neither happens automatically.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] The selected posts have comparable organic evidence and fit the goal.
- [ ] Up to three to five qualify without forced filler or invented proof.
- [ ] Original-post eligibility, media and audience conditions are checked.
- [ ] Combined budgets and individual end/review dates fit actual scope.
- [ ] Saved state, delivery and final verdicts are recorded separately.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example:** A shop has four recent helpful repair posts, but only two beat their comparable baseline and have clear music rights. The operator chooses those two, not a weak third to meet a quota. Their nominal seven-day plan is $14 total in an eligible setup, with actual platform limits checked. One test misses its goal and is paused under the agreed rule; the other is a candidate for a new dated continuation. The report does not call the second a proven sales winner from views alone.
