# Senior team member reviews weekly

A proposed fix needs a clear answer and a next step. This guide helps a reviewer judge the proof and wording each week. Start with the real queue and the current guides it affects.

**The path:** Queued changes → Evidence review → Decision → Verified release or return

**Start when:** The agreed weekly review is due and actual pending proposals need decisions or next actions.

## Inputs

- The actual proposal queue, prior pending decisions and review cutoff.
- The current affected guide revisions and the 500-word/four-part proposal standard.
- A named reviewer with decision authority and the supported change/publication workflow.

## Steps

1. Open the queue at the agreed review time. Record the pending scope and any inaccessible items. An empty view is not a proven empty queue until filters/access are checked.
2. Check format: problem, affected guide/section, exact wording and two real examples within 500 words. Return missing parts with a specific reason instead of pretending the request was substantively rejected.
3. Read the examples and current source. Judge whether the change fixes the problem and preserves adjacent steps, source authority and tool/method separation.
4. Record approved, rejected, returned or decision pending with reviewer, date and reason. Do not force every item to a final decision just to end with an empty queue.
5. For an approved change, use the authorized owning-source workflow. Record the version/change history and verify the saved/public result separately. An approval awaiting deployment stays approved, not released.
6. For returns/rejections, send the reason only through the authorized project channel and verify that notice. Keep the item findable and name any next action.
7. Flag aged pending items and repeated issues for the process owner. Preserve original submission dates and decision latency for later analysis.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

[Quality assurance (QA)](https://localservicespotlight.com/article-guidelines/) means checking the work against the agreed result.

- [ ] Every in-scope proposal has a justified decision or an explicit pending dependency.
- [ ] Approvals and actual releases are separate, with changed-version/readback evidence.
- [ ] Reasons and next owners remain visible; overdue work is not hidden.
