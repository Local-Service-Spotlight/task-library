# Schedule and assign quarterly auditors

A page can drift if no one owns its next check. This guide gives each page a reviewer and a real due date. Start with the current page list and people who can do the work.

**The path:** Current pages → Named reviewers → Agreed dates → Coverage check

**Start when:** A new quarterly audit window needs named reviewers and agreed page-level due dates.

## Inputs

- The maintained audit inventory, current register and prior unfinished checks.
- Reviewer availability, relevant source access and independent-review requirements.
- An agreed quarter, timezone and authority for assignments/calendar changes.

## Steps

1. Reconcile the article list from the current register and canonical index. Include existing gaps and page roles; the old guide’s teaching table is not the live inventory.
2. Assign one accountable reviewer per page, with needed specialist help. Balance actual scope and avoid an author being the only reviewer of their own changes.
3. Agree specific due dates and an audit window with those reviewers. Q3 2026 was the source’s first-cycle target; retain it as history rather than assigning an expired deadline to every new run.
4. Save assignments in the existing system. Include page URL, source revision, reviewer, due date/timezone, required checks and evidence location.
5. Create only authorized calendar reminders or jobs, check their saved details and confirm intended participants can see them. A saved event is not an accepted assignment or a completed audit.
6. At the midpoint and quarter close, compare planned checks with actual receipts. Carry misses with named owners and revised dates, keeping the old due date and reason.
7. Capture load or workflow problems from the real rotation and propose improvements. Do not erase missed audits by changing a status label.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

[Quality assurance (QA)](https://localservicespotlight.com/article-guidelines/) means checking the work against the agreed result.

- [ ] Every in-scope page has one accountable reviewer, clear scope and an agreed date.
- [ ] Saved assignments/reminders are verified; conflicts and access gaps remain visible.
- [ ] Coverage uses actual audit receipts, with unfinished checks carried forward honestly.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example. This is not a client result or proof of a completed run.**

A fictional quarter has 12 pages and two reviewers. One large guide is split into specialist checks but keeps one accountable reviewer. At close, 10 have complete receipts and 2 remain blocked on source access. Report 10/12 audited, not 12/12 because all were assigned.

## Run with an agent

Give the AI worker this recipe, the real Inputs above, the intended result and the actions already authorized. Ask it to return the saved output, checks, evidence and remaining owner. Check its work against this guide; loading a skill does not prove access, installation of a job, or successful execution. Keep media muted with volume at zero if playback is needed.

For recurring work, keep the actual trigger, owner and runtime in the job record. Scheduling and observed firings are separate. Do not create a schedule merely because this guide mentions a review interval.
