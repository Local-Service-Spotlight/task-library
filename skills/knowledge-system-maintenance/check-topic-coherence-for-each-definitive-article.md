# Check topic coherence for each definitive article

A page should help readers with one clear subject. This guide finds text, links, and media that send them elsewhere. Start by saying what the page is for, then check each part against that purpose.

**The path:** Page purpose → Every element → Relevance decision → Fix list

**Start when:** An article audit begins and its declared purpose needs to be checked against the whole page.

## Inputs

- Exact page URL and source revision, declared role and reader need.
- [current article guide](https://blitzmetrics.com/definitive-article-guide/) and the [SEO Tree, the map of related pages](https://blitzmetrics.com/seo-tree/).
- The page outline, media/transcript sources, link list and audit-record location.

## Steps

1. State the page’s purpose and role from its real body and opening. A task recipe teaches a repeatable job; a hub explains a subject; a reference or story has another role. An unclear purpose is a finding to resolve.
2. Inventory every section, image, video, example and meaningful link. For each, record how it supports the purpose; use captions/transcripts or silent frames for media, without claiming a sound review.
3. Check examples for matching subject and method. A related person’s photo proves that moment, not their use of the method or a customer result.
4. Check headings for a competing topic and links for useful parent, prerequisite or supporting context. A related prerequisite link can belong without turning the whole page into that topic.
5. Classify each item keep, clarify, move, remove or not verified. Give the exact location and reason. Preserve source evidence and prior history before proposing a move.
6. Apply authorized small corrections through the owning editor/source. For structural moves, prepare destination and cross-link changes together so useful material is not lost.
7. Save the checked inventory and open issues. Capture reusable lessons within 24 hours; a ticketed defect remains unresolved on the page until a fix is verified.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

[Quality assurance (QA)](https://localservicespotlight.com/article-guidelines/) means checking the work against the agreed result.

- [ ] The page’s purpose and role are explicit; all elements have dispositions.
- [ ] Every proposed removal or move has a relevance reason and preservation plan.
- [ ] Open defects remain visible with owners; tickets are not counted as repaired pages.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example. This is not a client result or proof of a completed run.**

A fictional bike-repair guide includes a video about a roofing company. The video has no lesson about bike repair, so the audit proposes moving it to its true topic. A link to the shop’s booking page stays because it is the reader’s next step. The move is not marked done until both pages are checked.
