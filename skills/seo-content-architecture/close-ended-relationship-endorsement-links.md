# Close endorsement links for an ended relationship

Keep your site clear about who you work with now. This guide checks old links when a work relationship ends, while keeping true history. Start with the agreed change and the pages your team can edit.

**The path:** Confirmed relationship change → Controlled-link inventory → Narrow edits → Public closeout check

**Start when:** The relationship owner has confirmed a specific ended relationship and the public association that must change.

## Inputs

- A dated source confirming the relationship’s end, the exact entities involved, affected roles and decision owner.
- The relevant controlled sites, public profiles and draft records, with current edit rights and release authority.
- Existing account/access ownership, historical facts to preserve and the [relationship closeout method](https://blitzmetrics.com/when-trust-ends-the-link-ends/).

## Steps

1. Confirm the actual event and scope with the owning record. Distinguish an ended agency relationship from a former employee role, an expired offer or an unsupported allegation. If the owner’s decision is missing, stage the inventory and leave the public change pending.
2. Inventory affected links and association claims only on surfaces the team controls. Save the exact URL, surrounding sentence, destination, role and current source. Use explicit searches and known site maps; an incomplete search is a bounded inventory, not proof that all web references were found.
3. Classify each item as current endorsement, active role, factual historical record, primary evidence or unrelated identity reference. The closeout removes misleading current association while retaining true history. An old event appearance or dated quote does not become false simply because the working relationship ended.
4. Prepare the smallest appropriate edit for each confirmed current endorsement. Remove a hyperlink, qualify the former role or revise its surrounding sentence as warranted. Never retarget the other party’s name to your own site and leave a misleading anchor; a new destination requires a truthful label and relevant purpose.
5. Keep account and infrastructure ownership separate. Remove only an explicitly authorized obsolete role or access grant after checking the correct account and recovery dependency. Do not delete the other party’s site, transfer their domain, lock them out or change unrelated administrators as a side effect.
6. Use the supported content rail for authorized edits, preserving current source, media and unrelated fields. Check the normal anonymous page and link behavior after save. Public caches or inaccessible profiles remain specific verification gaps; a successful save response alone does not prove the public association changed.
7. Save before/after evidence, unchanged history, inaccessible surfaces and each remaining owner. Any notice to the other party needs its own send authority. Complete the scoped closeout when its actual checks pass, without claiming control over copies or accounts outside the agreed boundary.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example. This is not a client result or proof of a completed run.**

A fictional agency stops managing a contractor’s marketing. Its team page still says the contractor is a current partner, while an old case study accurately names a 2024 project. The team removes the current-partner badge and clarifies its link. It retains the dated project story and records an external directory correction as pending, not completed.
