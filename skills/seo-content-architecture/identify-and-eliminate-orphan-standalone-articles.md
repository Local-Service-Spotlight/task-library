# Identify and eliminate orphan/standalone articles

Help readers find useful pages that your site has left out. This guide checks pages with no link leading to them and plans the right fix. Start with the full page list, then compare it with the links people can follow.

**The path:** All published URLs → Incoming-path check → Keep, connect or review → Fresh crawl

**Start when:** A full published-URL inventory and a bounded internal-link crawl are available for the agreed site.

## Inputs

- [Inventory pages and their purpose](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-1-inventory-content-and-establish-gct-per-page#task-step-1-inventory-content-and-establish-gct-per-page) with source and crawl coverage, including pages a link-only crawl may miss.
- [Map actual content roles](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=map-content-hierarchically-root-trunk-branches-leaves#task-map-content-hierarchically-root-trunk-branches-leaves) with actual page roles, parents, incoming body links and site navigation.
- Current content sources and valuable facts/media, search data if available, and authority for exact edits or removals.

## Steps

1. Reconcile the published CMS list, sitemap and crawl before flagging orphans. A crawler that follows only existing links can miss the very pages being sought. Record excluded content types, private pages and failed fetches, and keep inaccessible records unknown rather than treating them as zero-link pages.
2. For each published URL, distinguish incoming internal links, outgoing links and a mapped parent. A page with no detected incoming path is an orphan candidate within the measured scope even if its category or parent is known. Check both body links and usable navigation, recording which definition the count uses.
3. Read each candidate for reader purpose, unique evidence, business function and current relevance. Choose connect, improve, consolidate-review, remove-review or intentionally unlisted with a reason. Utility or campaign pages may have a valid limited role; absence from the main topic tree alone is not permission to delete them.
4. For a useful content page, add a natural incoming link from its relevant existing hub or other suitable page, with a short explanation of its value. Add the page’s own parent or service link where useful. An outgoing link from the orphan does not itself create an incoming route.
5. For overlapping or weak pages, pass unique material and an exact proposed disposition to [Review duplicate or weak pages](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=remove-duplicate-thin-pages#task-remove-duplicate-thin-pages). Redirect only when the reviewed replacement truly serves the old intent; no equivalent replacement may require a proper not-found response. Do not hide unresolved decisions behind an irrelevant nearest-hub redirect.
6. Apply only the authorized content and routing changes through their maintained sources, with current-source guards and retained media. Verify the real link in the normal rendered body and the destination’s actual response. A saved row or category reassignment is not evidence that a reader can reach the page.
7. Re-crawl the agreed scope and compare each original candidate with the new incoming paths. Report connected pages, deliberate exceptions, merges/removals and remaining failures separately. Update the content map and owner list; do not claim “zero orphans” when the inventory or rendered crawl remains incomplete.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

Quality assurance (QA) means checking the actual result against its agreed requirements. Follow the [Article Guidelines](https://localservicespotlight.com/article-guidelines/).

- [ ] The inventory includes unlinked published pages, and the incoming-link definition and coverage are stated.
- [ ] Each accepted connection is a verified incoming path from a relevant live page.
- [ ] Exceptions and destructive decisions have explicit reasons and authority; no blanket redirect or false zero-orphan claim is used.
- [ ] The exact output, source revision, reviewer evidence and remaining owner action are saved.
- [ ] For any reader-facing output, the short grade-five opening states the reader’s useful outcome and supporting method or proof. The body delivers that promise; a useful authentic visual appears in the first screen. Retain exact text and quoted reviewer evidence.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example. This is not a client result or proof of a completed run.**

A fictional site has a useful repair story and an unlisted thank-you page. Neither appears in the body-link crawl. The guide adds an incoming story link from the repair hub and checks it live. The thank-you page has an intentional campaign role and stays separately noted. The report shows one repaired content orphan and one justified exception.
