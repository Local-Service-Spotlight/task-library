# Step 3: Identify money pages and orphan pages

Plan links that help readers find your key pages. This makes useful posts and offers easier to reach. Use the checked [page list](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-1-inventory-content-and-establish-gct-per-page#task-step-1-inventory-content-and-establish-gct-per-page) to spot gaps before the next task places the links.

**The path:** Check the page list → Read both pages → Plan useful links → Hand off checked rows

A **money page** helps a reader buy, book or ask about a service; the name does not prove sales. The [money pages and blog posts guide](https://blitzmetrics.com/money-pages-vs-blog-posts/) explains their different jobs. An **orphan page** has no incoming internal link. Treat it as a candidate until the checked coverage supports that claim; an incomplete check can miss a real path.

**Start when:** The page inventory and supported taxonomy review are ready for an exact source-to-target link plan.

## Inputs

- [Inventory pages and their purpose](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-1-inventory-content-and-establish-gct-per-page#task-step-1-inventory-content-and-establish-gct-per-page) and [Review WordPress taxonomy](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-2-fix-categories-and-tags-in-wordpress#task-step-2-fix-categories-and-tags-in-wordpress) results, including actual incoming-path counts, link types, counted URLs, crawl/extraction method and date, known entry pages, exclusions and unresolved coverage gaps. A crawl follows links to inspect pages; it can miss pages with no path from its starting point.
- The live offer/service destinations, current business priorities and verified comparable revenue data only if connected.
- Read access to current source and target pages, dated source revisions and the maintained edit surface or its named owner. This task saves a plan; it does not need publishing access or permission to change a live page. Missing required source access stays a named gap.
- Reader purpose, body content and existing links for potential sources; the [Money Pages versus Blog Posts method](https://blitzmetrics.com/money-pages-vs-blog-posts/).

## Steps

1. Identify the actual buying, booking, service and lead-capture destinations from the site and business owner. A “money page” is an offer/action role; its name does not prove it currently earns revenue. Keep the live URL, intended action, source owner and any known duplicate or redirect.
2. Flag pages with no detected incoming internal route within the inventory’s stated scope. Cross-check the crawl against the published-page export so unseen pages are included. Record incoming body links and navigation/archive links separately. Zero body links with a working menu link is a contextual-link gap, not proof of an orphan. An assigned parent, an outgoing link or a sitemap listing alone is not an incoming link from another page. If access, rendering or crawl coverage is incomplete, keep the state “candidate—coverage incomplete” and name the missing check; do not report a site-wide orphan count. Record the actual counted source URLs, not just a tool total.
3. Prioritize offers with the owner’s current business goals, relevant proof gaps and measured support. Use verified comparable revenue only when available; otherwise label priority as a business decision, not a revenue rank. An expensive offer or a zero-link count alone does not establish financial impact.
4. For each target, read candidate source pages and their [Goals, Content, Targeting: the result, source material and people served](https://blitzmetrics.com/gct-business-strategy/). Select places where a reader would genuinely benefit from the next destination. Record the actual supporting sentence or section; reject keyword-only matches and avoid turning every source page into a link to every service.
5. For each useful orphan candidate, plan an incoming link from an existing relevant page that readers can reach from a known site entry, such as the home page or a linked topic hub. Check and record that path; links only between otherwise unreachable pages do not connect the group to the rest of the site. Add an outgoing parent link only where it helps readers. Route weak or overlapping content to its separate review task. A proposed incoming link is a plan, not proof of a repaired path; placement and QA must check the released result.
6. Write one action row per exact source-to-target relationship. Include a row ID, both URLs, checked final destination and date, source revision, exact sentence/section, proposed anchor and change, relevance reason, incoming coverage evidence, intended edit surface or its owner, and placement owner. Keep the proposed action (add, improve-existing, no-change) separate from its review state (proposed, accepted, held). The accepted row records who reviewed it and when. A missing hub, inaccessible source or unresolved offer duplicate stays held with a next owner/action; do not invent an edit location or use a misleading target.
7. Save the priority-offer list and orphan/exception list with one disposition for every in-scope candidate: accepted link plan, justified no-change, content review or held. Reconcile their counts to the input list so nothing disappears. Review the plan with its responsible owner and pass only accepted rows to placement within the existing authorization. For planned measurement, pass the target URL set, question, metric, time window, data source/access owner and baseline capture request before release; record captured proof or the actual gap. A missing baseline is not zero or invented historical data. Missing required scope or source evidence keeps the planning result partial. No row is called published or repaired by this task.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

Quality assurance (QA) means checking the actual result against its agreed requirements. Follow the [Article Guidelines](https://localservicespotlight.com/article-guidelines/).

- [ ] Money-page roles and business priorities are sourced; unknown revenue is not labeled zero or proven importance.
- [ ] Orphan candidates retain dated scope and evidence. Missing body links, navigation paths and incomplete coverage are distinct; no unsupported site-wide claim is made.
- [ ] Every in-scope candidate has a counted disposition. Planned incoming paths start from reachable relevant pages; no proposed change is called a published repair.
- [ ] Every accepted row has the exact source/target, checked target meaning, proposed context, current base revision, edit surface or owner, reviewer/date and placement owner. Held rows name a next action.
- [ ] The receiving worker can locate the intended edit and check it against the source revision; planned measurement has a baseline request and captured proof or an explicit gap.
- [ ] The exact output, source revision, reviewer evidence and remaining owner action are saved.
- [ ] For any reader-facing output, the short grade-five opening states the reader’s useful outcome and supporting method or proof. The body delivers that promise; a useful authentic visual appears in the first screen. Retain exact text and quoted reviewer evidence.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example. This is not a client result or proof of a completed run.**

A fictional firm has a roof hub linked from its home page, a roof-repair story, and a roof service page. A body-only crawl finds zero incoming links to the story. A menu check then finds a link, so the record changes to “body-link gap”; it does not call the story an orphan. The planner reads both pages and proposes a useful hub-to-story link. A story-to-service link is a separate row.

- **Row A — proposed add:** Roof hub → repair story. Place “see this roof repair” after the repair-method paragraph. Next, verify the exact URLs, source revision and edit surface, then get the row reviewed.
- **Row B — held:** Repair story → roof service. Place “roof repair service” after the repair result only once the service owner resolves the two competing offer URLs.

These are fictional labels, not usable URLs or accepted rows. A real row needs all fields in Step 6. If both candidate source and target sit outside any path from the home page, linking them to each other alone does not solve reachability. The owner may choose roofing as a business priority, but without verified revenue the plan does not call it the highest-earning branch.

## Handoff and Content Factory context

[Place the accepted links](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-4-create-links-with-proper-anchor-text-and-placement#task-step-4-create-links-with-proper-anchor-text-and-placement) receives the accepted row IDs, source/target URLs, exact sentence and proposed anchor, source revisions, edit-surface ownership, relevance/coverage evidence, reviewer/date and placement owner. It re-reads both pages before editing; a source or target change goes back for reconciliation rather than inheriting the old acceptance. [Review duplicate or weak pages](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=remove-duplicate-thin-pages#task-remove-duplicate-thin-pages) receives duplicate-intent cases; [Measure the accepted change set](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-6-measure-and-iterate-with-maa#task-step-6-measure-and-iterate-with-maa) receives the target set, question, metric, window, access owner and baseline request before release when measurement is planned. Its outcome analysis begins only after placement and QA provide the actual released log; sending a baseline request is not starting the measurement run.

This task supports the [Content Factory: Produce, Process, Post and Promote](https://blitzmetrics.com/content-factory/). The checked page list and grouping results are its inputs. Reviewing relevance and preparing the plan support Process. The accepted rows go to placement and QA in Post. A later measurement task checks observed changes; this plan claims no ranking or sales gain.
