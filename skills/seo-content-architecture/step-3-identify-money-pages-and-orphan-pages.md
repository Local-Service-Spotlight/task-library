# Step 3: Identify money pages and orphan pages

Find good pages that are hard to reach. This guide plans links to real offers and pages with no path in. Start with your page list, the offers you sell and the links the site now has.

**The path:** Actual offers + incoming gaps → Relevance review → Prioritized link rows → Placement owner

**Start when:** The page inventory and supported taxonomy review are ready for an exact source-to-target link plan.

## Inputs

- [Inventory pages and their purpose](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-1-inventory-content-and-establish-gct-per-page#task-step-1-inventory-content-and-establish-gct-per-page) and [Review WordPress taxonomy](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-2-fix-categories-and-tags-in-wordpress#task-step-2-fix-categories-and-tags-in-wordpress) results, including actual incoming-path coverage.
- The live offer/service destinations, current business priorities and verified comparable revenue data only if connected.
- Reader purpose, body content and existing links for potential sources; the [Money Pages versus Blog Posts method](https://blitzmetrics.com/money-pages-vs-blog-posts/).

## Steps

1. Identify the actual buying, booking, service and lead-capture destinations from the site and business owner. A “money page” is an offer/action role; its name does not prove it currently earns revenue. Keep the live URL, intended action, source owner and any known duplicate or redirect.
2. Flag pages with no detected incoming internal route using the inventory’s stated scope. Keep outgoing links, navigation links, body-link counts and assigned parent separate. Cross-check orphan candidates with the published source so unlinked content missed by the crawler is not excluded from the plan.
3. Prioritize offers with the owner’s current business goals, relevant proof gaps and measured support. Use verified comparable revenue only when available; otherwise label priority as a business decision, not a revenue rank. An expensive offer or a zero-link count alone does not establish financial impact.
4. For each target, read candidate source pages and their [Goals, Content, Targeting: the result, source material and people served](https://blitzmetrics.com/gct-business-strategy/). Select places where a reader would genuinely benefit from the next destination. Record the actual supporting sentence or section; reject keyword-only matches and avoid turning every source page into a link to every service.
5. For each useful orphan, plan an incoming link from an existing relevant page as well as an appropriate outgoing parent link. Route weak or overlapping content to its separate review task. A page becoming a source for an offer remains orphaned if nothing links into it.
6. Write one action row per exact source-to-target relationship, with proposed anchor/context, target status, relevance reason, source revision and owner. Mark add, improve-existing, no-change or held. A missing hub or unresolved offer duplicate needs its owner’s decision before a misleading target is used.
7. Save two coherent lists: priority offers needing support and orphan/exception dispositions. Review the plan with its release owner and pass only accepted rows to placement. Preserve missing access, unresolved source facts and baseline needs so the next task cannot mistake a proposed link for a published result.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example. This is not a client result or proof of a completed run.**

A fictional contractor has two service pages and a useful job story with no incoming link. The plan links the roof hub to that story, and the story to its relevant roof service. The owner chooses roofing as a current priority, but no revenue feed is connected. The report calls it an owner priority and does not label it the highest-earning branch.
