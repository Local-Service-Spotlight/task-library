# Step 6: Measure and iterate with MAA

See what changed when your new links went live. This guide compares real page data to help you choose what to do next. Start with the checked link list, its dates and the data you can access.

**The path:** Checked changes + baseline → Comparable page measures → Evidence-led analysis → Owned next action

**Start when:** A bounded linking change set has passed QA and has a defined measurement window and owner.

## Inputs

- [Check the placed links](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-5-qa-links-against-google-guidelines#task-step-5-qa-links-against-google-guidelines) accepted log with source/target URLs, release dates, held rows and exact source revision.
- Pre-change measurements when available, actual search/analytics access, consistent filters, timezone and comparable observation dates.
- The site’s agreed goals, actual offer actions and [Metrics, Analysis, Action: what happened, why and what to do](https://blitzmetrics.com/maa/) reporting format; known concurrent content, ad or site changes.

## Steps

1. Define the changed URL set, question, date range and success measure before interpreting numbers. Ideally save the baseline before Step 4 releases links. If that was missed, record the gap and use only available historical data; do not reconstruct a confident baseline from memory or a different site.
2. Collect comparable page/query search measures such as clicks, impressions and average position from the actual connected source. Collect relevant sessions or observed inquiries only when those systems are accessible and their definitions are known. Keep disconnected, filtered or delayed data unknown rather than zero.
3. Use consistent properties, periods, filters and URL handling for before/after comparisons. Keep search measures, sessions, link clicks and business outcomes separate; one is not a substitute for another. Note low sample size, partial weeks, seasonality and concurrent page, campaign or site changes.
4. Check whether the intended paths are still live and useful, especially former orphans and important offer links. A new crawl can establish incoming connections; it does not prove search indexing or a sale. Indexing observations and rank measures require their own source and date.
5. Write the analysis in terms the evidence supports. A rise after the edits is an observed association, not proof that one anchor caused it. Explain plausible competing causes and what further observation would distinguish them. Do not claim to identify which individual link “did the work” from a page-level traffic total.
6. Choose a bounded next action tied to the finding: repair a broken relevant path, improve a weak source, inspect a missing data source, retain a useful link or wait for adequate data. Do not change anchors or remove helpful links simply because a short window shows no traffic gain.
7. Write the report with actual metrics, interpretation, action owner, due point and evidence links. Finish this scoped review when its real checks pass, or leave it blocked on a named dependency. A continuing program uses its existing agreed job and review cadence; this recipe does not create a schedule or turn one execution into an endless counted loop.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

Quality assurance (QA) means checking the actual result against its agreed requirements. Follow the [Article Guidelines](https://localservicespotlight.com/article-guidelines/).

- [ ] Dates, URL scope, metric definitions and access gaps make the comparison reproducible.
- [ ] Observed changes are separated from causal claims, and traffic is not mislabeled revenue.
- [ ] The action follows evidence and has an owner; the bounded execution and recurring program remain distinct.
- [ ] The exact output, source revision, reviewer evidence and remaining owner action are saved.
- [ ] For any reader-facing output, the short grade-five opening states the reader’s useful outcome and supporting method or proof. The body delivers that promise; a useful authentic visual appears in the first screen. Retain exact text and quoted reviewer evidence.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example. This is not a client result or proof of a completed run.**

A fictional review sees more search clicks in the two weeks after six links changed. A new article and a paid campaign also launched that week, and inquiries are not connected. The report states the click change, notes those limits and keeps the helpful links. It assigns a later comparable review rather than claiming the links generated sales.
