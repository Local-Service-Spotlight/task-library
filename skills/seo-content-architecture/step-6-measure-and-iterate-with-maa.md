# Step 6: Measure and iterate with MAA

See what changed after your new links went live. This helps you choose which page needs work next. Use the [checked link list](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-5-qa-links-against-google-guidelines#task-step-5-qa-links-against-google-guidelines) to find the right pages and dates before you compare results.

**The path:** Check the changed links → Compare like with like → Explain the limits → Hand off the next action

[MAA means Metrics, Analysis and Action](https://blitzmetrics.com/maa/): what the numbers show, what may explain them, and what to do next. A **baseline** is the saved measure from before the change. A **measurement window** is the set of dates you compare.

**Start when:** A defined set of released links has passed quality assurance (QA), meaning the actual links were checked. The review has a question, time window and responsible owner. A pre-release baseline request can prepare inputs; it is not an outcome review of links that have not gone live.

## Inputs

- The [link QA task](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-5-qa-links-against-google-guidelines#task-step-5-qa-links-against-google-guidelines) accepted log: row IDs, source and target URLs, release dates, exact revisions, corrections and held rows. Only released, accepted rows enter the changed set; held rows keep their owner and reason.
- The agreed question, success measure and [Goals, Content and Targeting](https://blitzmetrics.com/gct-business-strategy/): the result wanted, material available and people served. Name the actual offer action, such as a recorded booking, without calling traffic revenue.
- Read access to the intended search or analytics property, or a dated export from its named data owner. A property is the site/account whose data you are measuring. Record its identifier, metric definitions, URL set, filters, time zone, dates and export time. No new permissions or live edits are required by this reporting task.
- Available pre-change data, the agreed review window and known concurrent content, ad, tracking or site changes. Missing access, source definitions or baseline proof remain named gaps, not zeros.

## Steps

1. **Fix the scope before reading the result.** Save a review ID, accepted change-row IDs, URLs, actual release dates, question, metric and agreed success rule. Request the baseline before placement where possible. If missed, use only retrievable historical evidence and label the gap. Do not choose a favorable date range after seeing the numbers.
2. **Check whether the data can answer the question.** Confirm the property and page/query filters with the data owner. Use equal-length periods with the same weekday mix where practical, and state the exact inclusive dates and source time zone. Exclude the rollout interval or explain mixed exposure; if links went live on different days, split the groups or disclose the difference. Check how recent data are marked and avoid treating an incomplete latest day as a full day. Seasonality and other changes can still make matching dates an imperfect comparison.
3. **Save the original evidence.** Keep the dated report/export, query or filter settings, definitions and collection time in the approved location. Record whether each needed value is observed, unavailable, preliminary or not comparable. Check unavailable marks in the original report before trusting export zeros. Keep search clicks, impressions, site sessions, link clicks, bookings and revenue separate. Search clicks count visits from search results; they do not count clicks on your new internal links. Read the [Search Console definitions](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/7576553?hl=en) when using that source.
4. **Compare only like measures.** For each URL or defined URL group, record both periods, values, units and source link. Show the absolute change (after minus before); show percent change only with a nonzero comparable baseline, and label a zero baseline as undefined for that calculation. Do not mix a property total with selected page rows, or add query rows and assume they equal a complete total. For rates, retain their counts and denominators; average position is its own source-defined measure, not a sales score. Mark small samples and changing definitions so a reader can judge the limits.
5. **Recheck the useful paths.** Open the normal source and destination pages from the accepted log and save the check date and result. A crawl can check incoming links within its stated coverage; it does not prove search indexing or a sale. A broken path goes back to the [link QA task](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-5-qa-links-against-google-guidelines#task-step-5-qa-links-against-google-guidelines) with the exact failed row and evidence. This report does not authorize a live repair.
6. **Write what the evidence supports.** State the observed change, plausible explanations and limits. A rise after edits is not proof that an anchor caused it. List competing changes such as a new post, campaign, tracking fix or seasonal demand. Do not name the individual link that “did the work” from a page-level traffic total. If the source cannot answer the question, say that; do not replace missing business results with traffic.
7. **Choose and hand off one bounded next action per finding.** Save the finding, action, recipient, due date or observable trigger, and the check that will show it is done. Options include a documented repair request, more source evidence, keeping a useful link, or a later comparable review. Do not remove helpful links merely because a short window shows no gain. Save the package below, have its reviewer check it, and record the receiving owner's acceptance or the exact pending dependency. A standing program uses its existing cadence; this guide creates no new schedule or endless execution.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

The output is a saved MAA report with its evidence and next-action list. Follow the [Article Guidelines](https://localservicespotlight.com/article-guidelines/) for reader-facing versions and keep private account data in its approved location.

- [ ] The report identifies its question, accepted row IDs, URL scope, release dates, source revisions and planned success rule. Every in-scope row is included or has a stated exclusion.
- [ ] Each comparison retains both periods, property, filters, time zone, definitions, units and source/export reference. The reviewer can reproduce the displayed change; any unavailable or noncomparable value stays labeled.
- [ ] Actual values, calculations and observations are separate from interpretations. No traffic number is relabeled a booking, revenue or proof that links caused growth.
- [ ] Current path checks and known concurrent changes are attached. A failed link retains its row, evidence and repair owner.
- [ ] Each finding has a next action, recipient, due point and acceptance check. The saved report revision, reviewer/date and receiving-owner acceptance or pending response are recorded.
- [ ] The opening explains what the reader can do, why it helps, and its linked input or next task. The body delivers that promise, with a useful visible visual; the review quotes the actual words.

A report can pass even when traffic fell or the supported next action is to wait. Missing required evidence or unaccepted handoff keeps the result partial or blocked on a named dependency. A finished report is not proof of a business gain, and an ongoing program is not one forever-running task.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example. These numbers are not a client result or a completed run.**

Six accepted links went live on June 15. The analyst compares June 1–14 with June 16–29: two equal 14-day windows, with the rollout day left out and the same property, page set, filters and source time zone. The source contains complete data for both periods.

- **Metrics:** Search clicks rose from 80 to 100: 20 more clicks, or 25% above the baseline. Inquiries are not connected, so the report says “unknown,” not zero.
- **Analysis:** A new article and a campaign also launched. The change does not show how many people clicked the internal links or prove they caused more sales.
- **Action:** Keep the useful paths. Ask the data owner for the agreed inquiry measure before the next review; the receiving analyst checks matching dates and definitions before accepting it. A separate offer page rose from zero to three clicks, so its percent increase is undefined, not 300%.

## Handoff and Content Factory context

The [weekly MAA reporting task](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=submit-weekly-maa-report-every-friday#task-submit-weekly-maa-report-every-friday) receives the report ID/revision, question, accepted change log, source/export references, comparison settings and values, calculations, path checks, limits and action list. Confirm that the receiver can open the approved evidence; a link to an inaccessible file is not a completed handoff. Record their acceptance or the missing response without sending outside existing communication authority.

The [link-planning task](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-3-identify-money-pages-and-orphan-pages#task-step-3-identify-money-pages-and-orphan-pages) receives supported new link proposals. Broken released rows go to QA; missing data go to its actual owner. Each receives only the relevant finding and evidence, with a due point and done check.

In the [Content Factory: Produce, Process, Post and Promote](https://blitzmetrics.com/content-factory/), placement and QA in Post supply the checked release. This review measures what happened afterward and feeds the next Process or Post repair, or a separately authorized Promote decision. It does not start ads or change live pages.
