# Record a One-Minute Thank-You Video

A short video can make your thanks feel personal. This guide helps a business owner thank one person for one real deed. Start with your notes and a phone. Finish with a clear clip that is ready to check.

**The path:** Checked note → short recording → checked clip.

**Use this when:** you have a specific reason for thanks and recording is suitable for the person and intended use.

## Inputs

- The checked note from [Find a Real Reason to Say Thanks](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=identify-thank-you-triggers#task-identify-thank-you-triggers).
- A phone or camera, a quiet place, enough light and a place to save the original file.
- The intended viewer and any limits on public use of the person’s name, story or image.

## First-run prompt

> Turn this checked note into three brief talking points for a thank-you video. Help me record and review one clear clip. Keep the person’s deed and my reason for thanks accurate. Save the original and a review copy.

## Steps

1. Use the note to say who you are thanking, what they did and why it mattered. Speak to the person. Aim for about a minute and one point; do not add a sales pitch.
2. Choose a quiet space with light on your face. Frame for the planned use, leave room for captions and clear private details from the background.
3. Make a short test to check framing and sound. An agent must keep playback muted with volume zero; use captions and audio metadata, or ask the human recorder to assess sound without starting speaker playback.
4. Record in your own words from the talking points. A natural take is useful, but redo a wrong name, unsupported claim, inaudible line or major interruption. One take is a preference, not a rule that overrides accuracy.
5. Save the original file before editing. Label it with date, person or safe project code and topic, without exposing private facts in a public filename.
6. Review the clip against the source. Check the name, deed, meaning, framing and intended duration. Use a transcript to catch errors; do not treat generated captions as proof that the spoken name is correct.
7. Save a checked review copy and the transcript. Record any sound check that a person still needs to make. Hand off the file and sharing limits; recording does not itself mean the clip has been sent or posted.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] The clip thanks one correct person for one sourced deed.
- [ ] A usable original and review copy are saved and open successfully.
- [ ] Framing and transcript are checked; sound verification and any limits are recorded.
- [ ] The message has no invented result, favor request or implied endorsement.
- [ ] Sharing scope and the next owner are attached to the files.
