# Capture client stories on phone

A happy client can explain your work in their own words. This guide helps you save that story with their name and clear permission. First, ask what changed and where they are happy for the story to appear.

**The path:** Permission → Client’s own story → Facts and files → Review.

**Use this when:** A real client has a specific result and agrees to tell the story on camera.

## Inputs
- A real client, a specific project or change, and evidence for any stated numbers.
- Permission to record and a separate clear record of allowed use of the person’s name, face, words, and company or location. Capture limits and the date.
- A phone, a quiet location, and permission to film the location and supporting shots.
- The approved raw folder, upload access, tracker, and named reviewer who will check facts and permission.

## First-run prompt

> Help me capture a client story from the facts and permission I provide. Draft plain interview questions, an attribution line, and a shot list. Flag claims that lack proof and uses that are not agreed. After capture, check the file package and prepare a factual handoff for review.

## Steps
1. Ask what may be recorded and where it may be used. Explain the intended article or channel. Record the answer in the project’s permission log; do not infer public use from a person agreeing to a private call.
2. Check the person’s full name and the right role, company, or city for the story. For a homeowner, do not invent a job title or company. Let the person confirm the attribution line.
3. Frame the person at eye level in a real setting. Check light, framing, and microphone. Keep private papers, addresses, or other people out of the shot unless cleared.
4. Ask four plain questions: What was the problem? What work was done? What changed? Who would this help? Let the client answer in their own words. Follow up on vague praise with “What is one example?”
5. When the client gives a number, note its source and what period it covers. If it cannot be checked, keep it as a claim for review or omit it from the finished piece. Do not turn a guess into a proven result.
6. Ask the client to confirm their name and attribution on camera when practical. Capture a still photo and about 30–60 seconds of supporting footage, called b-roll, such as the finished work. If consent or the setting prevents a shot, record that gap.
7. Name the video, photo, and supporting footage YYYY-MM-DD-clientname-story with a type or take suffix. Upload to the approved folder and log the links, permission, attribution, result evidence, and reviewer.
8. Send the raw package for review through [Upload the source to Drive and Descript](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=step-1-upload-video-to-google-drive-and-descript#task-step-1-upload-video-to-google-drive-and-descript). A missing name, usage permission, or unverified claim stays clearly flagged before any public use.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] The story describes a specific real change in the client’s own words.
- [ ] Full attribution fits the person and is confirmed; permission covers the proposed use.
- [ ] Claims have evidence or are marked unverified. A named quote alone does not prove a number.
- [ ] Video, a still image, and supporting footage are saved, or the exact capture gap is recorded.
- [ ] The reviewer can open the files and see all privacy or publication limits.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example — invented people and shop, not a testimonial to publish.** Morgan, a made-up customer of Maple Cycle, says: “I did not know if the bike was worth fixing. They gave me a written quote before doing the work, so I could choose.”

The teaching attribution is “Morgan Lane, local customer.” The sample story makes no sales or safety claim. Its evidence would be the approved quote and the customer’s confirmation. A shot of the quote would hide contact details and the home address.

The teaching permission note says “website story and shop social posts; no paid ads agreed.” That means an editor could prepare those drafts after a real consent check, but an ad would need its own authority and usage check. Do not copy this invented quote into a real client page.

## When this runs

Run for each willing client story when a real result exists. This task sets no automatic outreach or recurring filming schedule.

## Run with an agent

The agent can prepare the plan, organize allowed files, and check the evidence it can actually access. A person records real stories, supplies permission, or resolves an account step when needed. Save the current state in the project files and tracker. A model name does not create memory, access, a scheduler, or permission to act.

For all agent media checks, keep the player muted and its volume at zero before playback. If that cannot be checked, use captions, metadata, or still frames. Do not claim sound quality was heard when it was not.
