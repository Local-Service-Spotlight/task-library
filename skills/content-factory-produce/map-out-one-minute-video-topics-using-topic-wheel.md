# Map out one-minute video topics using Topic Wheel

It is hard to film when you do not know what to say. This guide helps an owner turn real questions and stories into a short video list. Start with what you sell and who it helps, then choose useful topics.

**The path:** Clear offer → Useful topics → True stories and questions → Video queue.

**Use this when:** The recording queue is empty, off-topic, or hard to link to the business offer.

## Inputs
- The current offer, intended customer, and owner’s real purpose and goals.
- Real source material: customer questions, call notes, comments, FAQs, allowed event questions, and true owner or partner stories.
- The current [SEO Tree](https://blitzmetrics.com/seo-tree/) map of owned topic pages, or a record that the page map is still missing.
- A sheet or drawing tool and edit access to the content tracker. Name the person who can approve the plan and the person who will record.

## First-run prompt

> Use my current offer, true stories, customer questions, and page map to build a Topic Wheel and recording queue. Put WHAT at the center, HOW topics in the middle, and WHY stories outside. Keep sources and permission notes. Do not invent people, relationships, demand counts, or page links.

## Steps
1. Use the current [Topic Wheel](https://blitzmetrics.com/topic-wheel/) definitions: WHAT is the product or service at the center; HOW is the middle ring of topics and expertise; WHY is the outer ring of people and true stories. The audience moves from stories toward the offer, but planning starts with the offer.
2. Write one clear sentence for WHAT. State the actual product or service and who it is for. Do not replace the offer with a vague mission line.
3. List about three to seven HOW topics that support the offer and match real expertise. Merge duplicates. Keep the owner’s plain words instead of invented framework names.
4. For each topic, collect real WHY stories, people, and allowed collaborations. Record the source and permission state. A wish to work with someone is not an existing relationship.
5. Add real customer questions under the matching topics. Preserve the question wording and source in the private tracker. Questions can produce teaching clips; they are not themselves the WHAT ring.
6. Match topics to the owned page map. Use the topic hub for the main explanation and specific stories as supporting pages when appropriate. Mark a missing page as a gap instead of linking an unrelated page.
7. Create one recording-queue row per useful prompt: question or story, source, topic, link to the offer, intended viewer, page home, speaker, and state. Rank by observed repeat questions and the agreed business need. If frequency was not measured, say so.
8. Review the queue with the plan owner. Hand accepted prompts to [Record one-minute videos](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=record-one-minute-videos#task-record-one-minute-videos) or an agreed [50-clip batch](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=batch-record-50-raw-clips-in-one-session#task-batch-record-50-raw-clips-in-one-session). Save the wheel, source list, and current queue together.
9. The source calls for a monthly refresh. Keep answered items linked to their clips; do not erase history. Add new questions and mark replaced or stale prompts. A calendar job needs an agreed owner, timezone, access, and trigger before it can run.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] The wheel uses WHAT center, HOW middle, and WHY outside, with the actual offer stated.
- [ ] Each selected topic relates to the offer; each story or question has a traceable source.
- [ ] The queue has a clear prompt, topic, speaker, and owned-page destination or named page gap.
- [ ] Priority is based on evidence or labeled judgment, with no fabricated demand counts.
- [ ] Accepted prompts and prior answers remain findable for recording and later review.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example — not a real business plan or customer research.** Maple Cycle’s sample wheel has WHAT at the center: “Bike repair quotes and service for local riders.” Its HOW topics are repair quotes, planning a shop visit, and used-bike questions. An outside WHY story is Alex’s wish to make basic questions feel welcome.

| Prompt | Ring or topic use | Sample source | Recording choice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Why did Alex open the shop? | WHY story | Invented founder note | One origin story |
| Can I send a photo before a visit? | HOW: planning a visit | Invented customer email | One teaching answer |
| What does a quote visit include? | WHAT: current offer | Invented service sheet | One offer explanation |

The sample does not say “customers ask this 20 times a week,” because no real count exists. In a real queue, replace each invented source with the actual record and set the next speaker before filming.

## When this runs

Create the first wheel once. The source proposes a monthly refresh and updates when new questions arrive. Treat monthly as a planned review until a real owner and schedule have been configured; no reminder is installed by this guide.

## Run with an agent

The agent can prepare the plan, organize allowed files, and check the evidence it can actually access. A person records real stories, supplies permission, or resolves an account step when needed. Save the current state in the project files and tracker. A model name does not create memory, access, a scheduler, or permission to act.

For all agent media checks, keep the player muted and its volume at zero before playback. If that cannot be checked, use captions, metadata, or still frames. Do not claim sound quality was heard when it was not.
