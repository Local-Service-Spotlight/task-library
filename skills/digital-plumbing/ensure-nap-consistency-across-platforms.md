# Ensure NAP Consistency Across Platforms

Old phone numbers can send people to the wrong place. This guide helps you keep your name, place and phone right on the web. Start with the facts for each shop you own.

**The path:** Trusted facts → Profile list → Scoped fixes → Public comparison.

**Use this when:** a business changes its name, location or phone, takes over old profiles, or finds conflicting public facts.

## Inputs
- An owner-backed identity record for each branch: public trading name, address or service-area policy, phone, website and supporting evidence.
- The in-scope website and profile URLs, with actual editing access or a responsible owner for each.
- Known legitimate differences such as branch numbers, approved call tracking, character limits and hidden service-area addresses.

## First-run prompt

> Compare the supplied public profiles with the business fact record. Fix true conflicts within scope and record legitimate platform differences. Keep private addresses private. Do not force every branch or tracking number to match one universal string.

## Steps
1. Write the trusted name, address and phone record. These facts are often called NAP. Use current business evidence and the owner’s confirmed public details; an outdated Google listing is not automatically the truth.
2. Separate distinct locations, brands and people before comparing. A second real branch may need a different phone or address. Identify the canonical website and proper profile for each entity.
3. List the actual surfaces in scope: website header/footer/contact/schema, Business Profile, social Pages and other important maintained listings. Record public URLs and who can edit them; missing access is a specific dependency.
4. Compare facts, not just characters. “Street” and “St” can describe the same address, while a wrong suite or disconnected phone is a real defect. Record acceptable formatting differences instead of generating cosmetic change work.
5. Preserve privacy and eligibility rules. A service-area business may hide its address on Google; do not publish a home address across the web just to make fields look identical. Apply the appropriate public facts on each platform.
6. Update true mismatches through each supported source within current authority. Preserve legitimate dynamic call-tracking behavior and record its relation to the stable business number. Do not change an authentication or recovery contact because a public phone changed.
7. Read back the public fields after save. Some platform edits are reviewed before going live; record submitted, pending and publicly visible states separately. Check structured facts and contact links alongside visible text on the website.
8. Save a platform-by-field comparison with evidence dates and next owners. Route the remaining exact mismatches, not a vague request to fix all listings. Keep old evidence for the run record while updating the maintained identity source.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] A trusted fact record exists for each real business or branch in scope.
- [ ] All scoped surfaces were compared, with legitimate formatting and privacy differences explained.
- [ ] Incorrect facts were fixed and read back, or carry exact pending state and owner.
- [ ] Tracking and private account contacts were not confused with public business identity.
- [ ] The record links to current public proof without claiming universal web-wide consistency.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example.** Oak Repair’s owner confirms its new shop at 14 Cedar Street. The website says “14 Cedar St,” which is the same address. Facebook still says “41 Cedar Street,” a true conflict. The guide changes the transposed number and keeps the harmless abbreviation. A service-area sister business has a hidden home address, so its blank public street field is marked intentional. The comparison teaches factual consistency rather than a character-matching score.
