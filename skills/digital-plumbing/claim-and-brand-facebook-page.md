# Claim and Brand Facebook Page

People may find an old or wrong Page when they search for your business. This guide helps you fix the right Facebook Page. Start by finding the Page your business should own and checking who can edit it.

**The path:** Find Page → Check control → Update facts → Public check.

**Use this when:** a business needs a Page, has an unclaimed listing, or needs its existing Page brought into line with current facts.

## Inputs
- The business identity record, canonical website and any existing Page IDs or URLs.
- A legitimate account with the needed Page permissions, or the actual owner who can provide them. Record access without sharing passwords.
- Rights-cleared logo, cover photo, business description, public contact details and the requested scope of changes.

## First-run prompt

> Find the existing business Page before creating one. Verify access and the real business facts, then complete authorized branding edits. Report pending access separately from public branding. Do not merge Pages, transfer control, send invitations or run ads unless those actions are included in the job.

## Steps
1. Search the known Page URL, business name and location. Record likely matches and why each is the same business or a distinct valid location, brand or person. Multiple results do not prove that they should be merged.
2. Open the actual Page management view and confirm the Page ID and current role. Meta distinguishes Facebook access from task access, and full control can manage permissions. An invitation or request is pending until accepted and usable.
3. If no Page exists, use Meta’s supported create-Page flow for the real business. If one exists but access is missing, use the supported request or owner-grant route within current authority. Keep a named dependency for missing control; do not create a competing Page as a shortcut.
4. Update the public business name, category, website and contact details from the identity record. Select an available clear handle. Preserve service-area address privacy and supported platform formatting rather than forcing identical characters everywhere.
5. Add the approved logo and cover image, checking crop and legibility on phone and desktop. Use real owned or licensed material; a fictional staff photo or implied customer endorsement does not belong on a real Page.
6. Write a short About section that says what the business does, whom it serves and how to reach it. Company voice may use “we”; do not force a company to speak as one individual or invent claims.
7. Choose an appropriate contact action supported by this Page and test its destination without sending an unrequested message. Link a business portfolio or Instagram account only when that connection serves the agreed workflow; do not create an ad account just for branding.
8. Save and read back the public Page and management view. Record edited fields, image crops, website destination and current control. Route duplicate merges, ownership transfers or access removals as separately scoped changes when needed.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] The exact Page represents the intended business; existing valid Pages were not treated as duplicates without evidence.
- [ ] The needed role works and business control is identified, or access remains a named pending item.
- [ ] Approved name, contact, website and images are saved and readable on both widths.
- [ ] Page actions lead to their intended destination; no unauthorized messages or ads were sent.
- [ ] Public branding and private account-control evidence are recorded separately.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example.** Oak Repair finds two results: its main Page and a valid Page for its second branch. The main Page still links to an old website. The guide changes that one link, replaces a cropped logo and sets the call action to the confirmed branch line. Three planned edits receive three public read-backs. The second branch stays intact. A colleague’s pending invitation is recorded as pending access, not “team access complete.”
