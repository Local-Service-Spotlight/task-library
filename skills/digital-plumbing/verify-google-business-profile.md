# Verify Google Business Profile

People need to find the right business on Maps. This guide helps you set up and check its Google page. Start with the real facts and any page that is there now.

**The path:** Eligible business → Existing profile → Offered proof → Verified state.

**Use this when:** an eligible business needs verification, has uncertain profile control, or receives a reverification request.

## Inputs
- The actual business activity, public trading name, appropriate address or service-area details and phone, supported by current records.
- The exact existing Profile ID or URL and legitimate business-controlled Google access, or the authorized setup/control-recovery scope.
- The owner or operator who can supply the real proof Google requests, using the method offered to that profile. Keep identity documents and private footage out of public evidence.

## First-run prompt

> Find the correct eligible Business Profile and inspect its control and verification state. Complete the supported method Google offers within current authority. Preserve pending review, legitimate ownership and private evidence; do not create duplicates or claim Maps visibility proves verification.

## Steps
1. Check business eligibility against current Google representation rules. An online-only personal brand does not qualify just because it has a website. Use the real customer-facing or service-area business model and preserve address privacy where required.
2. Find existing profiles by the known business name, location and supplied ID. Distinguish the intended business from legitimate branches or unrelated names. Do not create a second profile to bypass a missing-access problem.
3. Inspect actual management access and verification state. If the profile exists, use the supported claim or access-request route as needed. If none exists and it is eligible, create the correct profile within authorized scope. A public Maps listing can exist without your verified control.
4. Confirm business-controlled ownership and the operator’s actual role. Review unfamiliar accounts with the owner; do not automatically remove or demote people just because their names are new to this agent. Transfers and removals use the specific existing authority and supported process.
5. Complete the verification method or combination Google offers. For required on-site video or other business proof, use real current evidence and follow the actual prompts. Do not fabricate a storefront, manipulate location proof or substitute stock footage. Keep agent playback silent.
6. Save the submission state and time without exposing verification codes or private records. Google may review the evidence and request another step. Pending is not verified; do not promise that waiting a fixed number of days will finish it.
7. Reopen the management view and verify the actual confirmation or outstanding request. If the process fails, record the provider’s stated reason and use its supported correction or appeal route. A model cannot force verification through endless retries.
8. Compare public business facts and Maps display with the approved identity record, allowing legitimate formatting and service-area differences. Record management verification, public visibility and account control as separate results and hand off exact open items.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] The business is eligible and the exact appropriate profile is used without a bypass duplicate.
- [ ] Business control and operator role have actual evidence.
- [ ] Google’s requested proof is genuine, private where needed and submitted through its supported process.
- [ ] Verified, pending, failed and public-visible states are recorded distinctly.
- [ ] Public business facts match reality without forced private-address exposure or invented ranking claims.
