# Stage 2: Goals (mission, 90-day goals, CPA/ROAS, budget)

Choose a goal your business can afford and serve. This guide puts the next three months of marketing on one clear page. Start with real sales, costs, lead quality and the budget the owner has agreed to.

**The path:** Business facts → Goal + economics → Bounded budget → Checked brief

**Start when:** The accepted engine scope needs a goals and economics brief before content, targeting or spend.

## Inputs

- [Tracking and access findings](https://local-service-spotlight.github.io/task-library/?task=stage-1-plumbing-fb-ads-google-ads-analytics#task-stage-1-plumbing-fb-ads-google-ads-analytics) and the accepted business scope.
- [Goals, Content, Targeting: the result, proof and people for this work](https://blitzmetrics.com/gct-business-strategy/) source, mission, offer, audience and current service capacity.
- Revenue, relevant delivery costs, lead-to-customer rate, refund/collection timing and approved marketing budget from the responsible owner.

## Steps

1. Write the mission and one clear business outcome. Use the actual offer and customers; do not copy a broad purpose statement when the source needs a specific result.
2. Set dated goals for the next 90 days using the current baseline and capacity. Keep projected leads, qualified leads, booked work and collected revenue separate.
3. Define the result in cost per acquisition (CPA) before using it: a lead or a customer produces different math. Use the owner’s allowable acquisition cost after relevant costs and margin needs. Average collected revenue per customer times the verified lead-to-customer rate estimates revenue per lead; it is not a profitable cost-per-lead ceiling.
4. For a lead plan, an explicit planning ceiling can be allowable customer-acquisition cost multiplied by the verified lead-to-customer rate, with consistent definitions. Record uncertainty and who accepted the economics; do not invent the missing close rate.
5. If using return on ad spend (ROAS), state the conversion value divided by ad cost and what the value represents. [Google’s definition](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6268637?hl=en) targets an average value-to-spend ratio; it does not guarantee revenue or profit.
6. Check the plan on the same period: approved 90-day spend divided by assumed cost per lead estimates leads, then apply the same qualified/close-rate definitions and capacity. Show a scenario range and the shortfall if the plan cannot support the goal.
7. Save the one-page goal, assumptions, limits and owner’s decision. Changes to the budget, scope or target require their existing authority; an attractive forecast is not permission to launch.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example. This is not a client result or proof of a completed run.**

A fictional owner allows $100 to acquire one new customer, after its cost review. One in four qualified leads becomes a customer, so the planning ceiling is $25 per qualified lead. A $750 test could yield 30 such leads if that cost holds. These are assumptions to test, not promised sales or an instruction to spend.
