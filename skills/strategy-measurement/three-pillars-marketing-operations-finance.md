# Three Pillars (Marketing, Operations, Finance)

See which part of the business needs help first. This guide looks at sales, the work you deliver and the money you keep. Start with the owner’s goal and one useful number for each part.

**The path:** Business goal → Three functions → Supported constraint → Review plan

**Start when:** A whole-business review must locate a supported constraint before reallocating effort.

## Inputs

- The accepted goals, current roles and recurring work.
- Sourced sales/pipeline, delivery/quality and finance records for comparable periods.
- The owner and relevant function leads, with the scope of decisions they can make.

## Steps

1. Define the three functions from the source: Marketing/Sales creates demand and sales; Operations delivers the promised work; Finance plans and tracks money and resources. These are functional lenses, not a newly certified reconstruction of the nine-part diagram.
2. Map in-scope work and roles to their primary function, retaining real cross-functional dependencies. A task that spans functions is not automatically irrelevant or a delete candidate.
3. Choose a useful measure and target for each function. Examples are qualified pipeline, on-time delivery with quality, and margin or available cash. The responsible owner must define the metric and source rather than accepting a generic dashboard label.
4. Compare the values with the goals and operating limits. Distinguish a missing measure from a weak measured result. Check whether the proposed constraint actually limits the business, using supporting records.
5. Draft a concrete response for the best-supported constraint, with owner, time/budget limits and expected signal. A staffing, pricing or financial decision follows its existing authority; this review does not make it automatically.
6. Save the functional map, evidence and unresolved questions. Set the agreed review condition and carry the three functions into the relevant weekly or strategic report.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example. This is not a client result or proof of a completed run.**

A fictional shop has enough booked work but misses three of ten promised dates. Its cash figure has not been supplied. The review investigates delivery capacity first while keeping finance unknown. It does not declare finance healthy or hire another person just because the operations metric is weak.
