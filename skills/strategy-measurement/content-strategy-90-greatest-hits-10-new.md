# Content Strategy (90% Greatest Hits, 10% New)

Get more from content that has worked well. This guide helps your team plan mostly proven content, with some room for new tests. Start with a list of past work and its results.

**The path:** Content + results → Greatest hits → 90/10 plan → Review

**Start when:** A content or promotion plan needs a split between proven work and new experiments.

## Inputs

- The content inventory with current rights, URLs, dates and evidence for its results.
- The planned period, effort capacity, approved budget and business goals.
- [Topic Wheel, the map of your offer, topics and real stories](https://blitzmetrics.com/topic-wheel/) and the previous plan or review.

## Steps

1. Use the source’s 90/10 principle as a planning target for effort: about 90% on proven greatest hits and 10% on new tests. Name the unit being allocated, such as hours, assets or approved spend; do not blend these into one misleading percentage.
2. Group comparable assets by their job and judge them against that job. Reach or watch time may support an awareness asset; qualified leads support a lead goal. Mark old or missing evidence for recheck.
3. Build the greatest-hits pool with each item’s proof and current fit. Confirm its claim, offer, rights and destination are still usable; past success does not guarantee future success.
4. Allocate reuse actions: refresh the existing canonical page, recut a clip, or prepare paid distribution under [Dollar a Day, small paid tests of proven content](https://blitzmetrics.com/dollar-a-day/). Connect reused assets to their parent on the [SEO Tree, the map of related pages](https://blitzmetrics.com/seo-tree/) rather than creating competing thin pages.
5. Choose a small experiment list from real questions and gaps. For each test name the hypothesis, variable, limit, review window and success rule before results arrive. Publication and spend stay within their separate authority.
6. Prepare the calendar with owner, source, output and review date for each item. Compare planned effort and any authorized spend to the intended split; record a justified exception rather than fabricating a 90/10 match.
7. At review, compare actual allocation and results. Promote a new item into the greatest-hits pool only when its relevant evidence meets the agreed test; a generic median from unlike assets is not a valid gate.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

Quality assurance (QA) means checking the result against its agreed requirements; see the [Article Guidelines](https://localservicespotlight.com/article-guidelines/).

- [ ] The hit pool has current supporting evidence and usable source assets.
- [ ] The plan states its allocation unit and shows the intended split or a clear exception.
- [ ] Every experiment and reuse action has a scope, owner and review rule.

## Start with an agent

Give the AI worker this recipe, the real Inputs, the desired result and the actions already authorized. Ask for the saved output, source evidence, passed and failed checks, and the next owner. A skill is a set of instructions. Loading it does not prove access, install a scheduled job or complete the work. Use the [current installation guide](https://localservicespotlight.com/install/) only if reusable setup is needed; a ZIP contains instructions and is not an access grant.

Use the actual supported app and verified source access. A model name is not a promise of browser control, memory or unattended work. For recurring work, inspect the real job’s trigger, owner, timezone and observed firing; this recipe creates no schedule. Before any media playback, mute the player and set volume to zero. A silent review does not prove sound was heard.
