# Create collaborative content with industry peers

Make one useful piece with someone whose work fits yours. This guide helps you plan a shared talk or article that serves both groups of readers. Start with a real topic you can teach together.

**The path:** Real topic + peer → Agreed brief → Shared source → Checked releases

**Start when:** Two relevant peers may want to make a useful joint source or article.

## Inputs

- [Topic Wheel, the map of your offer, topics and real stories](https://blitzmetrics.com/topic-wheel/) and a verified peer/audience shortlist.
- The proposed format, useful reader outcome, real proof and a small clear ask.
- Recording or writing access, participant consent, edit/reuse rights and the actual release owners.

## Steps

1. Choose a peer for topic and audience fit. Inspect real work that shows why the person can contribute. Proximity to a recognizable name does not establish an endorsement or guarantee transferred trust; name the useful lesson the shared work will teach.
2. Draft a concrete proposal: topic, audience, format, time needed and intended use of the output. Reference the peer’s actual work. Keep outreach as a draft until sending is authorized; track the real reply rather than inventing agreement from a warm relationship.
3. Agree the source format and rights before recording or co-writing. Specify who speaks or writes, who checks facts, who owns the files, which excerpts may be reused and which channels each person actually intends to use. Shared participation does not imply unlimited rights.
4. Capture the conversation or draft with one clear topic and genuine contributions. Save the original files, date, participants and source notes. An AI worker can prepare prompts and process supported sources; it cannot substitute for the people’s own experience.
5. Prepare the agreed outputs through [Content Factory: Produce, Process, Post and Promote](https://blitzmetrics.com/content-factory/). Keep each person’s meaning, verify names and facts, and review the short opening and meaningful visual under [Article Guidelines](https://localservicespotlight.com/article-guidelines/). Do not require both parties to publish every derivative if that was never agreed.
6. Use [entity linking, choosing the right home for a named person or topic](https://blitzmetrics.com/entity-linking/) for truthful citations to each person’s verified home and relevant work. Check actual links after authorized release. A useful editorial cross-reference is different from exchanging links chiefly to manipulate ranking.
7. Give the peer a specific thank-you under the agreed privacy and send rules. Record participation, published outputs and any actual praise separately in the proof inventory, with the exact permitted reuse and remaining handoff.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

Quality assurance (QA) means checking the actual result against its agreed requirements. Follow the [Article Guidelines](https://localservicespotlight.com/article-guidelines/).

- [ ] The shared source and each output follow the agreed topic, participants and rights.
- [ ] Every claimed release, credit and link is observed; pending peer work stays pending.
- [ ] The proof record describes the actual collaboration without invented endorsement or guaranteed reach.
- [ ] The exact output, source revision, reviewer evidence and remaining owner action are saved.
- [ ] For any reader-facing output, the short grade-five opening states the reader’s useful outcome and supporting method or proof. The body delivers that promise; a useful authentic visual appears in the first screen. Retain exact text and quoted reviewer evidence.
