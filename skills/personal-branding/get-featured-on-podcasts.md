# Get featured on podcasts

Share your useful work with people who listen to the right shows. This guide helps you plan a guest pitch and follow the episode through release. Start with one show and a topic its listeners need.

**The path:** Audience fit → Specific pitch → Agreed recording → Released episode and reuse

**Start when:** The person has real expertise and a suitable show may benefit from a guest conversation.

## Inputs

- A verified show list, actual recent episodes and audience fit.
- Approved bio, real headshot, home site, topics from [Topic Wheel, the map of your offer, topics and real stories](https://blitzmetrics.com/topic-wheel/) and proof the person can discuss them.
- Outreach authority, guest availability, recording/use consent and the show’s actual submission route.

## Steps

1. Read or inspect a real recent episode and the show’s guest process. Prioritize audience fit and useful subject matter, not just a promise of backlinks. Record the host, source, topic opportunity and any published submission requirements.
2. Prepare a compact pitch kit with current bio, photo, a few relevant talk ideas and verified source links. Explain what listeners will learn. Avoid unsupported performance numbers, borrowed credentials or an assumption that a famous previous guest endorses the new one.
3. Draft a personal pitch tied to the actual show and one clear topic. Send only with the existing authority and retain the exact thread. Track drafted, sent, replied, accepted and booked states separately; no-reply remains no-reply.
4. Once accepted, agree time, format, useful talking points and recording/reuse rights. Confirm which host controls the recording and release. Prepare the person to answer directly with true examples, and give the host the correct name and home URL.
5. Record the actual appearance under consent. Save the date, participants and available source file. An appointment is not a recorded episode, and the guest’s file is not proof the host has published. Keep private or embargoed material out of the public queue.
6. When the episode airs, verify the normal episode URL, identity, title and any show-note credit. Request a factual correction through the actual host if necessary; an editorial decision not to link the site stays recorded rather than overwritten as a completed link.
7. Prepare permitted clips, a site proof entry and a specific thank-you using their respective tasks. A neutral interview proves participation; only actual praise becomes a positive mention. Retain pending host release and downstream reuse as distinct handoffs.
