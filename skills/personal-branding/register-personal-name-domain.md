# Register personal name domain

Give your work a web address you control. This guide helps you choose and register a name people can connect to you. Start with the name you use for work and the account that will own the domain.

**The path:** Approved name → Available choice → Owner-approved purchase → Domain receipt

**Start when:** The person needs a name domain or verification of an already-owned domain before a site build.

## Inputs

- The accepted professional name, legitimate variants and the person’s preferred existing domain if any.
- An owner-controlled registrar account, purchase/renewal authority and budget.
- Any existing site, mail or DNS dependencies; the actual host plan and secure access-record location.

## Steps

1. Confirm the name and reader-facing identity before searching domains. Inspect any existing owned domain and links first; a site may already have a suitable stable home. Do not move an established site merely to match a spelling preference without a reviewed migration reason.
2. Check the preferred name domain through the actual registrar and record availability and quoted purchase/renewal prices. The house preference is a memorable personal-name .com; it is not a guarantee of higher ranking or a reason to pay an unapproved premium.
3. If needed, propose legitimate name variants such as a middle initial or full middle name. Check for confusion with a different person or brand and show the exact spelling. The owner chooses the actual domain; the recipe does not invent identity facts to fit availability.
4. With purchase authority, register in the person’s controlled account and verify the exact domain, term, receipt and owner access. Helpers use supported delegated access. Keep credentials and payment details in the approved vault, not a public task record.
5. Confirm renewal settings, current expiry date, contact verification and privacy options actually offered for that domain. Use the owner’s approved renewal policy; do not purchase extra years or claim privacy is available in every case merely to satisfy a checklist.
6. Prepare the next hosting and DNS handoff from actual provider instructions. Registration is separate from web routing, email setup and a live website. Preserve existing records and require the relevant change authority before pointing the domain or altering nameservers.
7. Save the selected spelling, registrar and DNS-provider roles, owner-access proof, expiry/renewal state and exact next action. A successful purchase is complete domain registration; resolving to the final site is a later deployment check.
