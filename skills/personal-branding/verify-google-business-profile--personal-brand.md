# Verify Google Business Profile

Help people find your local firm. Use this guide to check if your firm can have a profile on Google. Start with the way you serve your clients.

**The path:** Eligibility → Existing listing → Owner verification → Accurate public business facts

**Start when:** A qualifying business or eligible individual practitioner needs its Google Business Profile checked or verified.

## Inputs

- The actual business/practitioner, customer-contact model, location/service area and current business website.
- Business-owner authorization and Google account access, existing listing if any, and supporting business evidence.
- Current [Google’s business eligibility rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en) and [representation rules](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en).

## Steps

1. Check eligibility before opening a listing. Google generally requires in-person customer contact during stated hours; online-only personal brands do not qualify simply by having a website. Assess any practitioner under the actual public-facing and contact rules, not just the owner’s title.
2. Search for the existing business and distinguish it from the person’s Knowledge Panel. Confirm the right location, business and current ownership. Use the supported ownership-request path if another account manages it; do not create a duplicate to bypass the actual owner.
3. Use the real-world business name and accurate customer-facing contact details. Point the listing to the authoritative website for that business, not automatically the owner’s personal home. Apply the current address/service-area rules and hide a non-customer-facing address when required.
4. Follow the verification method Google actually offers in [its business verification flow](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en). The business owner supplies required evidence or completes human identity steps. A postcard, phone, email or video option is not guaranteed for every account.
5. Keep owner control with the real business owner and use the minimum permitted helper role. Record account role and verification state privately. Do not use a public-looking badge or a helper’s recollection as proof of completed verification; inspect the actual managed profile state.
6. Prepare accurate categories, hours, description, service information and real photos under the listing’s actual rules. Do not add keywords to the business name, fake locations or unverifiable claims to make the listing look complete.
7. After authorized save, verify both the managed verification state and the normal public listing’s identity and facts. Pending verification stays pending. A correctly reasoned not-applicable result is a legitimate output; it is not a failed personal-brand project or permission to create another kind of profile.
