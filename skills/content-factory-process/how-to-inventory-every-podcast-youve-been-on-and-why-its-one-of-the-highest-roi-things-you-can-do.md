# How to inventory every podcast you've been on

Your past interviews can help people learn what you know. This guide brings them into one list so your team can find and use them. Start with your name, known shows, and any list you already have.

**The path:** Name variants → Discovery leads → Verified appearances → One master list.

**Use this when:** A person needs a consolidated verified appearance inventory across podcast and video sources.

## Inputs
- The person’s verified name/variants, known shows, companies and public identity pages.
- Existing inventory and authorized supplied internal records, with private details kept out of public exports.
- Read access to Listen Notes, Podchaser, YouTube and show pages; account/profile changes have separate scope.
- A master sheet, date/scope, verification statuses, content-use rights and next owner.

## First-run prompt

> Merge the supplied and discovered podcast leads into one source-backed appearance inventory. Verify this person’s role, combine mirrors of the same episode, and keep mentions and uncertain identities separate. Record coverage, rights and metric gaps. Prepare a useful reuse queue without inventing appearances, contact permission or ROI.

## Steps
1. Set up one master record per real appearance with title, show, host, date, role, canonical source, platform links, audio/video availability, verification evidence and next use.
2. Search known names and variants in Listen Notes, Podchaser, YouTube, show websites and the person’s site. Record each source/query/date and any access or coverage limits.
3. Use supplied private archives only within the task’s scope. AI search can suggest leads but must retain a real source URL for verification. Do not turn a claimed result count into a confirmed total.
4. Verify that this person actually appeared and in what role. A mention, same-name person or quote repeated by the host is not a guest appearance. Keep uncertain leads in a review queue.
5. Merge directory mirrors, audio/video copies and reuploads of the same episode into one appearance with multiple links. Use show/title/date/participants/content evidence, not title similarity alone. Separate genuinely different appearances even if they share a show.
6. Record source media availability and permitted reuse separately from presence. A verified guest credit does not grant download, editing or advertising rights.
7. Prioritize useful verified material with comparable observed evidence and the real GCT. Top 20% is a method option with an explicit eligible denominator; unavailable engagement is UNKNOWN, not zero and not a fabricated ranking.
8. Keep hosts and collaborators in a sourced relationship list when useful, without inferring friendship or permission to contact them. Do not claim, edit a profile or send outreach merely because it was discovered.
9. Return the deduplicated inventory, source coverage log, verified/rejected/unresolved totals and priority handoff. If monitoring is part of the job, reuse an authorized alert or named review cadence, with the first result checked before claiming it works.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] One master row represents each verified appearance, with preserved platform links.
- [ ] Role/identity evidence separates appearances from mentions and same-name collisions.
- [ ] Source coverage, rights and metric gaps are explicit.
- [ ] Totals reconcile and monitoring/outreach states are truthful.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example — no real appearances counted.** Listen Notes finds a sample “Alex on Shop Talk” episode. YouTube has the same conversation under a shorter title, and the host site links both. They become one appearance with three links.

A different episode says “Alex once told me…” but has only the host speaking. That is a mention, not a second guest appearance. The sample sheet records one verified appearance and one rejected mention, with a third same-name lead awaiting review. It does not claim a complete lifetime total from those searches.
