# Write email newsletter from video key insight

A short email can share one point your readers can use. This guide helps you turn a video lesson into a clear note. Start with the source, the reader, and the article you want to share.

**The path:** Source insight → Clear email → Checked destination → Draft or verified send.

**Use this when:** A processed video/article has one useful insight for an existing opted-in audience and the job calls for an email draft or send.

## Inputs
- The [Goals, Content, and Targeting](https://blitzmetrics.com/gct-business-strategy/) brief, full source and checked article or clearly marked draft.
- The real sender/brand voice, email platform, intended list/segment and existing send scope.
- The current template, sender/reply settings, opt-out footer and suppression rules; keep the platform’s existing required controls.
- The campaign history, canonical destination, test recipient when authorized, and measurement owner.

## First-run prompt

> Draft one useful newsletter from this video and GCT in the real sender’s voice. Check the canonical article, intended audience and existing email controls. Complete tests/sends already authorized, then report exact campaign and delivery states. Keep drafts and scheduled sends distinct; do not invent opens, clicks or revenue.

## Steps
1. Choose the single lesson the source supports. State the reader’s useful takeaway before asking them to click. Do not invent a dramatic cost or customer result for the subject line.
2. Draft a few short specific subjects, then choose one that matches the body. Write an opening in the actual sender’s supported voice; company voice need not pretend the sender lived the video speaker’s experience.
3. Deliver the lesson in three to six short paragraphs or a compact list. Preserve the source’s limits and make the email useful even without a click.
4. Add one primary editorial call to action, usually the full owned article. Required footer/opt-out links still remain. Verify the final public canonical URL before a live send; a private draft is not a reader destination.
5. Place the draft into the approved template when in scope. Check names, reply identity, links, images, mobile layout, readable text and existing footer/suppression behavior. Do not change the list or broaden recipients from a previous send by assumption.
6. Send an authorized test to the named test address and inspect the received copy when that step is in scope. A platform preview is useful but not proof of inbox delivery. Keep test and production campaign IDs distinct.
7. Carry out the send or schedule if already authorized after checks pass; otherwise return the complete draft. Record the exact list/segment, chosen time zone, campaign ID and saved state. A scheduled item needs a named first-firing receiver and delivery-state check.
8. Read the platform’s actual post-send results and record sent/accepted, bounced or pending states accurately. A sent-folder copy proves dispatch, not delivery. Later opens and clicks are platform-reported signals, not proof of human reading or revenue.
9. Log the source, article, campaign and observed metrics/date for the existing [Metrics, Analysis, Action review](https://blitzmetrics.com/maa/). Keep unavailable metrics UNKNOWN and give the next owner the specific follow-up.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] One useful source-backed lesson and honest subject are present.
- [ ] Sender, intended audience, footer and final public destination are checked.
- [ ] Draft, preview, test receipt, scheduled and actual send states are distinct.
- [ ] Metrics and handoff use observed evidence without duplicate-send or business-result claims.
