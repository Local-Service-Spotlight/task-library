# Boost one-minute videos for personal branding

You have a short clip that people found useful. This guide helps you test it with more people who may need your help. Start with the real post and the proof that it worked.

**The path:** Proven clip → right people → small test → checked result.

**Use this when:** A real personal-brand post has earned useful organic response and the owner has a defined paid-test goal.

## Inputs
- The original post URL, recent organic metrics and the person’s actual Page or eligible professional account; role access to the connected ad account.
- A clear audience and goal from [Goals, Content, and Targeting](https://blitzmetrics.com/gct-business-strategy/), an approved creative and claims, and the full account/currency/budget/date scope for any paid test.
- Rights to the footage, people, music and testimonial; captions or an accurate transcript, a checked profile and a working destination when used.

## First-run prompt

> Review these clips and choose only those with real signal and clear rights. Prepare the exact account, original post, audience, budget, end date and success measure for a small personal-brand test. Carry out only the paid actions already authorized and report saved settings separately from actual delivery.

## Steps
1. Rank the person’s recent clips against their own baseline. Select up to three to five that answer a buyer’s real question, show useful work or contain a verified proof moment. Use fewer if fewer qualify.
2. Keep the person and company identities clear. Use the actual authorized identity that owns the post. A Public Figure category is not a universal requirement for every Meta ad, and a company Page does not become a personal Page by changing its label.
3. Check the opening, captions and visible proof with sound off. Retain the person’s actual words and context. A shared stage or photograph proves only that moment, not friendship, client status or endorsement.
4. Name the goal before choosing settings: relevant attention, profile visits or a defined business action. Choose the current eligible objective and the matching reported metric; do not count a view as a lead.
5. Use the supported existing-post option where available and verify the original post ID in preview. Confirm format, music and placement eligibility. If the original cannot be promoted, record that limitation before preparing a distinct derivative.
6. Build a small relevant audience within real service or market limits. Read whether the interface treats an input as a boundary or a suggestion. Do not assume a list or a famous person’s name guarantees delivery to those people.
7. Plan the method’s $1-per-day, seven-day test where the platform accepts it. Confirm minimums, budget type, actual spend limits, fees and combined tests before launch. If the account cannot fit the approved amount, leave that test pending; do not silently raise the budget.
8. For an authorized launch, re-read the saved identity, post, dates and budget. Check delivery and spend early, then review the planned full window with the same target. Brand-name searches or new followers can be supporting observations; they do not prove the ad caused a sale.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example:** A bike shop owner posts a 55-second tip on fixing a loose seat. It gets 12 useful questions and shares, while similar recent clips usually get three. She chooses a seven-day awareness test with a nominal $7 plan in an eligible account. The preview uses her real post and readable captions. At review, the operator records $6.80 spend and the platform’s actual view count. Two shop inquiries are recorded separately; neither is called an ad sale without matching evidence. The next test is proposed within a new dated budget, not left on by default.

## When this runs

One seven-day test per chosen clip. A series can add one qualified post per day within an agreed total budget. Monthly brand reviews or new tests require an actual recurring arrangement; this file creates none.
