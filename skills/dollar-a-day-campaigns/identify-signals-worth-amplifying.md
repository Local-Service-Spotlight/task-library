# Identify signals worth amplifying

You do not need to guess which post to boost. Look for posts that people already found useful. This guide helps you make a short list from your own results before you spend on ads.

**The path:** Recent posts → fair baseline → useful proof → short list.

**Use this when:** The team needs proven content for a small paid test or a fresh winner shortlist.

## Inputs
- The account’s available recent organic posts and metrics, ideally 60–90 days, with post URLs, dates and formats.
- The business goal, audience, ordinary baseline and content/media rights.
- Evidence for names, claims and results; current ad eligibility and a place to save the shortlist. Read-only exports are enough for this task.

## First-run prompt

> Rank these posts against their own account’s comparable baseline. Show the numerator, denominator, sample size and useful response behind each candidate. Select only proven, relevant, usable posts. Return the shortlist and gaps without spending, posting or inventing missing metrics.

## Steps
1. Collect the available recent organic history. Record the actual date range and any missing posts or metrics. Separate already-paid results so paid reach does not masquerade as organic proof.
2. Group comparable formats and goals. A 20-second clip and a long interview need different watch-time context; different platforms may define a view differently.
3. Compute a consistent engagement measure where reach exists: (reactions + comments + shares) ÷ reach × 100. Label this an interaction rate, not a unique-person rate. If reach is zero or missing, do not invent a ratio.
4. Compare with the account’s own median for a comparable group. The source’s two-to-three-times baseline can be a shortlist heuristic, not a universal win threshold. If the median is zero or the sample tiny, show absolute counts and that limit.
5. Read the actual responses. Questions, useful shares and relevant discussion may matter more than low-effort reactions. Remove spam or irrelevant response from the qualitative argument; do not secretly rewrite the source export.
6. Check whether each post helps the stated audience and goal. Keep proof tied to its source moment. Do not upgrade a co-appearance into a customer story, endorsement or close relationship.
7. Verify that the original post, media rights, captions, destination and current ad rules allow the proposed use. A strong post with an unresolved claim or rights issue remains on hold for paid use.
8. Choose up to three to five qualified candidates, with the evidence, reason and intended goal for each. If none qualifies, return that finding and a content task. Test fresh ideas organically first; do not divert an assumed 10% of paid budget into unproven content.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] Dates, paid/organic split and comparable groups are recorded.
- [ ] Every ratio has the correct denominator and a sample-size limit.
- [ ] Each candidate has useful response and goal fit, not only a popularity rank.
- [ ] Claims, rights and ad eligibility gaps remain explicit.
- [ ] The short list can be reproduced from linked post records; no budget action occurred.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example:** A shop’s ten recent repair clips have a median interaction rate of 2%. The seat-fix clip has 20 reactions, six comments and four shares on 500 people reached: 30 ÷ 500 × 100 = 6%. That is three times the median. The reviewer reads the comments and finds real repair questions. A joke gets more likes but no buyer relevance, so it is not picked for this goal. The seat clip goes on the shortlist only after its music and claims are checked.
