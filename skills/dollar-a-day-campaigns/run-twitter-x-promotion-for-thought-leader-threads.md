# Run Twitter/X promotion for thought leader threads

A thread can share a useful idea beyond your own fans. This guide helps you test a strong post on X with a clear cost limit. Start with a post that earned real replies or shares.

**The path:** Strong thread → fitting audience → bounded promotion → measured response.

**Use this when:** A person’s published thread has useful organic evidence and a paid X test fits the stated goal.

## Inputs
- Original thread and first-post URLs, recent comparable metrics and permission to promote this person’s content.
- The actual eligible X Ads account, ad role and billing scope; account-specific minimums, currency and dates.
- Goal, target, audience hypothesis and creative rights; a checked destination and the tracking actually needed for a website goal.

## First-run prompt

> Review this thread’s organic evidence and prepare an X test with the original entry post where eligible. Verify the actual audience mode, budget limits, dates and metric. Do not promise delivery to a named person or their exact followers. Apply only the actions already authorized and report the real saved state.

## Steps
1. Read the whole thread and its evidence, then compare replies, reposts, bookmarks or profile actions with the person’s comparable recent posts. Do not select a viral argument that does not serve the stated business goal.
2. Check account and post eligibility in the authorized interface. Verify the ad identity and rights to the words, images and linked material. A platform or account restriction is not permission to open a substitute identity.
3. Write the goal and matching result: relevant engagement, a defined profile action or website traffic. A website tag is not required just to count every platform-reported click; use the tracking needed for the actual downstream outcome.
4. Choose the first post when it gives a clear entry to the approved thread and is eligible for promotion. Read the promoted preview and linked thread. Keep the source URL; do not promise every reader opens the rest of the thread.
5. Use relevant interests or follower look-alike inputs offered by the platform. X describes look-alikes as people with similar interests to an account’s followers, not an exact list of those followers. Keep the actual geography and other supported controls in the log.
6. Read how targeting options combine. Multiple audience inputs may broaden a group rather than require every condition. Record overlap and the exact selected settings; do not sum overlapping interest reports as unique people.
7. Set the smallest supported test that fits the approved amount and dates, using the current budget level and controls. If $1/day is not available, name the real cost and leave an out-of-scope test pending. Avoid unnecessary edits but still check early errors and spend.
8. At the seven-day due review, compare the matching cost and useful response with the target. Report observed follows or inquiries separately from claimed attribution. Route the exact next action; do not double the budget or launch the next thread by default.

## Definition of done (QA checklist)

- [ ] Original thread, entry post, rights and paid eligibility are documented.
- [ ] Goal and metric match; click, follow and sale are not interchangeable.
- [ ] Follower look-alikes and overlapping audience reports are described correctly.
- [ ] Real account budget controls and dates fit the authorized total.
- [ ] Read-back and full-window verdict are saved with evidence limits.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example:** A shop owner’s thread on safe bike checks gets 18 useful replies, above her normal range of two to four. The plan uses the original first post and a relevant cycling-account look-alike input. It says “people with similar interests,” not “all followers of this rider.” The test log shows actual cost per relevant engagement at review. One journalist later follows the account, but the report does not claim the ad reached that person or caused the follow without evidence.
