# Create YouTube campaigns using ADUCATE model

You have a video that teaches one useful thing. This guide helps you test it as a YouTube ad. Start with the viewer’s problem and the result you want the test to measure.

**The path:** Useful video → fitting format → bounded campaign → measured result.

**Use this when:** A proven teaching video fits the campaign goal and the owner wants a paid YouTube test.

## Inputs
- Exact Google Ads account, YouTube video/channel, required account roles and link permissions for the chosen features.
- Approved video, media rights, captions, destination and goal; conversion setup only when that goal needs it.
- Currency, dates, allowed budget, success target, campaign type and test log; the source’s ADUCATE label does not supply an acronym definition.

## First-run prompt

> Use this approved teaching video and goal to prepare a bounded YouTube test. Verify the current format, campaign-level budget and reporting fields. Keep the documented teaching structure, and do not invent an expansion of ADUCATE. Execute only authorized steps and distinguish draft, delivery and outcome.

## Steps
1. Start with the owned Dollar-a-Day method: useful content with organic proof, then a small test. Write the viewer’s question, the lesson, its evidence and one fitting next step. The linked owned source does not define every letter of ADUCATE; do not invent those definitions.
2. Confirm the video is available on the correct YouTube channel and usable in the chosen ad. Review captions, factual claims, rights and the destination. Keep the opening useful instead of inserting an unsupported promise.
3. Choose a current campaign type and format for the goal. For skippable in-stream, the viewer can skip after five seconds, so put the point early. In-feed and Shorts work differently; do not apply a five-second skip rule to every format.
4. Choose supported audience and location settings that fit the offer. If using viewer remarketing, check the actual format, audience eligibility and permissions. A view does not guarantee that person can enter a retargeting list.
5. Set the budget at the campaign level. An ad group can have its own bids or targeting, but it does not provide a separate Google Ads budget. Separate budgeted tests need an appropriate campaign structure, with overlap and total commitments checked.
6. For a fixed test, consider the supported campaign-total option when creating a new campaign. It has a total amount and end date; existing campaigns cannot simply switch budget type. An average daily budget is flexible and may spend more on a day, so do not label $1/day a hard $7 weekly cap.
7. Before an authorized launch, re-read dates, networks/placements, objective, bidding, budget and video ID. Check early delivery and destination behavior with media silent. Do not click your own paid ad to test a landing page.
8. At the due review, use the actual format-specific metrics. Keep Google Ads TrueView views, public YouTube views, paid watch time and organic Studio metrics distinct. Report cost for the matching result; missing retention stays unknown.

## Example(s)

**Fictional teaching example:** A repair video opens with a loose seat and shows the fix. The planner chooses a supported skippable format and a new campaign with a $14 total budget over seven days, because that amount is the approved test. It is labeled a $14 test, not renamed a $1/day test. At review, the account reports $12 spend and 240 TrueView views, so cost per TrueView view is $0.05. That does not establish 240 unique people, full-video completions or new customers. The next step depends on the written goal.
